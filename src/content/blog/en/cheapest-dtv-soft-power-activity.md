The **cheapest DTV soft power activity** isn't always Muay Thai, and it isn't always the one gyms push hardest. Three options used to get compared for the DTV's soft power route: Muay Thai, a Thai cooking course, and a Thai language school program. Only two of those still qualify. Thai language schools were removed from Thailand's official soft power list in 2025, and applicants who want to study Thai are now directed to the Non-Immigrant ED visa instead.

I'm Meaw. I own and run **Wildcat** Muay Thai in Chiang Mai, so I have an obvious reason to want you to pick Muay Thai. I'm not going to pretend otherwise here. But candidates come to me every month having already ruled out cheaper options simply because nobody told them those options existed, or because they're working from an outdated list. This article is the honest comparison I'd want if I were choosing myself, current rules included, with no thumb on the scale for the activity I happen to run.

For the full cost picture beyond the course itself, embassy fee, agent or no agent, start with [the real cost breakdown of the whole DTV visa](/blog/dtv-visa-cost-breakdown). This article stays focused on one layer: comparing the **DTV soft power options** by price.

## Muay Thai, cooking, and language school: what actually still qualifies

**Muay Thai** and Thai cooking courses are the two soft power activities most commonly used for the DTV today. Thai language schools no longer count. As of 2025, general language schools were removed from the qualifying soft power list, and applicants who want to study Thai are now directed toward the Non-Immigrant ED visa instead, according to [The Thaiger's Destination Thailand Visa guide](https://thethaiger.com/guides/visa-information/thailand-digital-nomad-visa), a change confirmed independently by [ExpatDen's 2026 DTV requirements guide](https://www.expatden.com/thailand/destination-thailand-visa/). If you researched the DTV a year or two ago and saw a language school listed as an option, that information is out of date now.

The DTV's Thai Soft Power category itself covers a wider list than most people assume: Muay Thai, **Thai cuisine**, medical treatment, seminars, and art events, according to the same two sources. Cooking and Muay Thai remain the two activities with the clearest pricing and the most established paper trail for applicants, which is why the rest of this comparison focuses on them.

Muay Thai is [one of the officially recognised soft power activities](https://www.muaythaivisathailand.com/soft-power-and-the-destination-thailand-visa-for-muay-thai-students/) for the DTV. If you're set on Muay Thai specifically, [the DTV visa for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) covers everything about that route in detail, including how it compares to the ED and tourist visa for training purposes.

What both currently qualifying activities share: an enrolment document from the school or camp, sometimes an attendance record, and a genuine commitment to actually show up. Neither functions as a shortcut around the embassy's own review of your full file.

## Real price comparison: Muay Thai vs cooking vs language

**Cooking courses** and Muay Thai are the two soft power activities you can actually compare on price today. Language school pricing is included below for context, since it still shows up in older guides and forum threads, but it isn't a route you can use for the DTV anymore.

| Activity | Typical price | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Cooking course | Around **10,000 THB** | Qualifies |
| Muay Thai | **7,500 to 25,000 THB** | Qualifies |
| Language school | Around 7,500 THB (historical) | Removed from the list in 2025 |

Between the two qualifying options, cooking sits at a fairly fixed price point, while Muay Thai spans the widest range of any soft power activity, from the same low end as cooking up to more than double at the top. That gap has almost nothing to do with the paperwork itself. Some gyms bundle the enrolment letter into a mandatory long training package, some charge a separate admin fee for the letter alone, and some simply set a higher rate for anyone who mentions "DTV." We go through exactly why that gap exists, and how to spot the difference between a fair price and an inflated one, in [our full breakdown of gym pricing for DTV papers](/blog/muay-thai-gym-dtv-overcharging).

At Wildcat, the enrolment letter is included in the normal [training packages](/classes#pricing): 5,000 THB a month for unlimited group training, 7,000 THB a month for the **Fighter program**. No separate DTV line item. That doesn't make Muay Thai the automatic cheapest pick, at the top of its range it clearly isn't. It just means the price shouldn't move on you the moment you mention a visa.

What the price comparison doesn't capture: Muay Thai gives you a physical practice on top of the visa paperwork, several hours a week of actual training, not just a signature on a letter. A cooking course qualifies you the same way on paper, but the day-to-day experience is genuinely different. If checking the DTV box as cheaply as possible is your only goal, cooking is worth a serious look. If you actually want to train, that difference might matter more to you than the gap in price.

## How long does the course actually need to be?

The honest answer applies to both qualifying activities equally: six months or more is the length Thai immigration practice has favored through 2025 and 2026, but it isn't a published legal minimum for either one. Shorter enrolments, one month in particular, tend to draw more scrutiny regardless of which soft power activity you choose.

Nothing about this is specific to Muay Thai. An embassy reviewing a one-month cooking course enrolment reacts about the same way it would to a one-month Muay Thai enrolment: with more scrutiny on the rest of the file, proof of funds, travel history, stated plan. We cover this in more depth, including the exact reasoning behind it, in [the article on Muay Thai gym pricing and enrolment length](/blog/muay-thai-gym-dtv-overcharging).

The practical takeaway: don't choose the shortest, cheapest-looking course of either activity and assume duration alone secures approval. A coherent, complete file matters more than shaving a few weeks off any one course.

## Applying for the DTV without active income

You can apply for the DTV soft power route without active income, since the requirement is **proof of funds**, not proof of a job. This matters for retirees, early-retirees, and anyone living off passive income who assumed the DTV was only for remote workers with a paycheck.

The soft power category was built precisely for people who aren't working: you show **500,000 THB** in personal funds, held for at least three months, and a genuine enrolment in a qualifying activity, and that's the core of the file. There's no requirement to demonstrate ongoing income, a job offer, or an employer letter for this route specifically. ## FAQ on DTV soft power activity costs

### What are the cheapest soft power activities for the DTV visa?

Between the two currently qualifying options, cooking courses are generally cheaper at a fairly consistent price, commonly reported around 10,000 THB. Muay Thai ranges from 7,500 to 25,000 THB depending on the gym, so its cheapest option can undercut cooking, but its most expensive option is by far the priciest of the two. Language schools, once a cheaper third option around 7,500 THB, were removed from the qualifying list in 2025.

### Do I have to choose Muay Thai for the DTV, or can I pick another activity?

You don't have to choose Muay Thai. The DTV's soft power category also recognises Thai cooking courses, medical treatment, seminars, and art events. Language schools were removed from the qualifying list in 2025, so studying Thai no longer works as a soft power route; the Non-Immigrant ED visa is the current path for that instead.

### Does the DTV soft power course need to last a certain number of months?

There's no published legal minimum, but six months or more is the length immigration practice has favored through 2025 and 2026, for either qualifying activity. Shorter enrolments face more scrutiny as part of your overall file, not as an automatic rejection trigger.

### Can I apply for the DTV without a job or active income?

Yes. The soft power route requires 500,000 THB in proof of funds and a genuine enrolment in a qualifying activity, not proof of employment or ongoing income. It's a common path for retirees and anyone living off passive income.

### Does a Thai language school still qualify for the DTV visa?

No. As of 2025, Thailand removed general Thai language schools from the DTV's qualifying soft power list. If studying Thai was your plan, the Non-Immigrant ED visa is the current route, not the DTV soft power category.

You land in Chiang Mai in December, you want a **monthly scooter rental in Chiang Mai**, and every shop you message says "ask for availability". That is normal from November to February. This guide explains what changes in high season, what to check before you hand over any money, and five places we would use. It comes from Wildcat, a family-run Muay Thai camp in Hang Dong, so we are a rental option ourselves and we are first on our own list. We tell you why, and we are fair to the others.

Every fact below comes from a page we opened on 14 November 2026, and each competitor detail is linked to the page it came from. Prices and terms change, so confirm with the shop.

## What changes in high season (November to January)

We rent bikes ourselves, so we see it every season: **from November to January, prices go up and monthly rentals get hard to find.** In peak weeks, many shops only rent by the day or the week, at rates less favourable than their low-season monthly deals. You rarely see this on a price list: the shops we read (Mango Scooters, Cat Motors, Fleetcnx, Skoot) each show one price per period and quote high season per date. What their own pages and other guides do show:

- Cat Motors, an established shop, writes on its own page: "During high season, from November to February, please contact us beforehand to check availability." It adds that it usually confirms advance bookings within three days of the start date, and that outside high season no advance booking is needed.
- Grape Ride Rental says in its FAQ that advance booking is recommended in peak travel seasons.
- A shop blog, JeJe Motorcycles (July 2025), says prices "tend to go up quite significantly, especially during December and around holidays", without giving figures.
- A 2024 rental guide quoted one shop's monthly rate at 1,800 THB in low season and 2,000 THB in high season. That is one shop, two years ago, but it shows the direction.
- A Chiang Mai property agency says high season "can, however, see Chiang Mai sell out of rental motorbikes almost completely". It is undated and a single source.

Our reading: expect to pay more, expect fewer monthly offers, and expect the bike you wanted to be gone if you message late. That is the same season described in our guide to the [best time to train Muay Thai in Thailand](/blog/best-time-train-muay-thai-thailand): cool mornings, dry weather, and everyone arriving at once. Before the smoky months, see [the burning season in Chiang Mai](/blog/burning-season-chiang-mai).

## Monthly or weekly? Why long rentals go first

From a shop's point of view, a bike rented by the day often earns more than the same bike rented by the month, so in busy weeks the monthly rental is the easiest to turn down. That is what we see in peak weeks: shops would rather fill a bike day by day. Cat Motors says its daily, weekly and monthly rentals are "subject to bike availability", and most of its models show monthly rates "on request". That is its own page, not a rumour.

What does a monthly rental cost? Across the guides we read, a 110 to 125 cc scooter is usually around 2,500 to 4,500 THB a month. Published examples from shops: Mango lists a Click 125i at 2,900 THB and a Scoopy 110i at 3,500 THB a month. Our own published monthly rate is 3,000 THB. Larger bikes cost more.

If you are staying a month or more, message early, give exact dates and ask three things: is the monthly rate confirmed for those dates, what is the deposit, and what happens if the bike breaks down.

## What to check before you rent

**Paperwork and deposit**

- **Passport.** Do not leave your passport as security. The Irish Department of Foreign Affairs says: "Do not hand over your passport as security for vehicle hire." The UK foreign office says the same. Whether it is illegal for a shop to hold it is disputed, and practice varies: some shops take a copy, some will hold the original if you choose. A copy plus cash is the safer route.
- **Cash deposit.** For a normal scooter, shops we read ask roughly 1,000 to 5,000 THB, and more for bigger bikes. Have the amount written on the contract.
- **Damage.** Film a slow walkaround of the bike with the shop's staff in the frame before you take the keys, and photograph the odometer and fuel gauge. Ask to see the registration book. The UK foreign office warns that hire bikes in tourist areas are often unregistered and cannot legally be used on the road.
- **Contract.** Get the terms in writing: deposit, fuel rule, return time, and who pays for what.

**Insurance**

- Thailand's compulsory insurance, called Por Ror Bor, covers bodily injury and death only. It does not cover the bike. According to the insurance site Motorist, medical cover is up to 30,000 THB at first and up to 80,000 THB once fault is established, and death or total disability is covered up to 500,000 THB.
- Ask the shop what it covers for the bike and what your excess is in baht. Wording like "fully covered" tells you little.
- Your travel insurance may not pay if you ride without a valid licence, according to the UK foreign office. Read the exclusions.

**Licence, helmet and police**

- You need your home motorcycle licence plus an International Driving Permit that includes the motorcycle category, or a Thai licence. A car-only permit does not cover a scooter, and a Thaiger guide warns insurers may treat you as unlicensed. The UK government asks for the 1968 version of the permit. We found no Thai official text on which convention applies, so check with the body that issues your permit.
- Wear a helmet. Since 1 June 2025, press reports say the fine for riding without one is up to 2,000 THB for riders and passengers.
- Checkpoints are common around Chiang Mai. Blogs report licence fines of 500 to 2,000 THB, but we found no official figure, so treat that as a rough guide.
- **Fuel.** Most shops use a full-to-full rule, with a refuel fee reported at about 100 to 200 THB if you bring it back low.
- **Locks and theft.** We found no reliable norm. Ask in writing who pays if the bike is stolen, and always lock the steering.
- **Return.** Agree the return time and condition, and take a photo when you give it back.

Ireland's government goes further and strongly recommends not riding a scooter in Thailand at all. That is a fair thing to weigh, especially if you have never ridden. If you are new, start on a light automatic and keep to quiet roads for the first week.

## FAQ: scooter rental in Chiang Mai in high season

### Do I need to book ahead from November to January?

Yes, ideally. Shops such as Cat Motors and Grape Ride Rental tell customers to contact them first in high season, and monthly bikes are the first to run short.

### How much does a monthly scooter rental cost in Chiang Mai?

For a 110 to 125 cc scooter, guides and shops suggest about 2,500 to 4,500 THB a month. Larger bikes cost more. In high season, many shops only quote by the day or the week, so a monthly deal is harder to get: ask for the price for your exact dates.

### Can I use my passport as a deposit?

We advise against it. Irish and UK government advice both say not to hand it over. A copy plus a cash deposit is the usual alternative.

### Do I need an International Driving Permit to rent a scooter?

You need a licence valid for motorcycles. For foreign visitors that means your home motorcycle licence with an International Driving Permit that includes the motorcycle category, or a Thai licence.

### Can I get a scooter when I land?

Some shops deliver to hotels, such as Grape Ride Rental. With our Stay & Train offer, the airport transfer and a bike are included.

## Conclusion

In high season, the rental that works is the one you arrange early. Ask for exact dates, keep your passport, film the bike and check the insurance. If you plan to train in Chiang Mai, we can sort the bike with the rest of your stay.

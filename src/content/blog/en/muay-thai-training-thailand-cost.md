## What Muay Thai training actually costs

Training fees are the core of your budget. Here's the real range across Thailand.

### Per session (drop-in)

The **per-session rate** in Thailand ranges from **300 to 600 THB**. Northern Thailand camps, including those in the Chiang Mai and Hang Dong area, typically sit at the lower end of that range. Phuket camps, particularly the large international operations near Chalong, charge closer to 550–800 THB per session (~$16–23 USD).

At Wildcat, drop-in sessions start at 400 THB. See [our training rates](/classes#pricing) for current pricing (I don't reprint the full rate card here because it changes seasonally).

Practically: if you're planning a short visit of one to two weeks, drop-in is fine. For a month or longer, a monthly package is far better value.

### Monthly unlimited training

Most camps offer unlimited training packages. Here's the market range as of 2026:

- **Budget / northern Thailand camps**: 8,000–12,000 THB per month
- **Mid-range camps in Phuket or Bangkok**: 12,000–18,000 THB per month
- **Premium all-inclusive operations** (Tiger Muay Thai, Fairtex Bangkok): 15,000–25,000 THB per month for training alone

Worth knowing: **most camps offer a 10–20% discount** for commitments of three months or longer. If you're planning a serious training block, ask about long-stay rates when you enquire. It's standard practice and camps expect the question.

### What monthly training typically includes

A standard monthly package at a Thai camp covers:

- Two sessions per day (morning and afternoon)
- Padwork with a trainer in most sessions
- Bag rounds, clinch drilling, and technique work
- Group sparring (varies by camp culture)

What is usually charged separately: private one-on-one sessions, strength and conditioning classes, MMA or BJJ cross-training access, and any gym facilities beyond the Muay Thai area. Factor these in if they're part of your plan.

## Accommodation near a Muay Thai camp

Rent is the second-largest cost. Location matters: suburban camps like those in **Hang Dong** (south of Chiang Mai city) are in a lower-rent zone than the trendy Nimman area. That difference is real, sometimes 3,000–5,000 THB per month for a comparable room.

### Budget accommodation (Chiang Mai area)

- Studio room or shared house near a suburban camp: **4,000–7,000 THB per month**
- Shared facilities, basic furnishings, adequate for a focused training trip
- Many fighters and long-stay trainees choose this tier to stretch their training budget

### Comfortable accommodation (1-bedroom condo, private)

- Hang Dong / Chiang Mai suburbs: **7,000–12,000 THB per month**
- Nimman city centre: 12,000–20,000 THB per month
- Phuket (near Tiger Muay Thai / Sinbi): 10,000–18,000 THB per month
- Bangkok (near Fairtex): 12,000–22,000 THB per month

Wildcat works with nearby condo options for trainees who want accommodation close to camp. See the [training packages at Wildcat](/stay-train#packages) for details on what we arrange.

A practical tip: the 10–15 minute rule. If your accommodation is more than 15 minutes from your camp by scooter, you will lose training time and motivation over a long stay. Proximity to the gym matters more than neighbourhood.

## Food and daily expenses

Chiang Mai is one of the most affordable cities in Southeast Asia for food. Street market meals cost 60–80 THB. Sit-down restaurants for foreigners run 150–300 THB per meal. If you eat street food most of the time (which most long-stay trainees do), your food costs stay low.

A realistic breakdown of **daily living expenses in Chiang Mai** as of 2026:

| Item | Low budget | Comfortable |
|---|---|---|
| Street food / market meals | 60–80 THB/meal | 80–150 THB/meal |
| Monthly food estimate | 6,000–8,000 THB | 9,000–14,000 THB |
| Scooter rental | 2,500–3,000 THB/month | 3,000–4,000 THB/month |
| Utilities (electric, water) | 1,000–2,000 THB/month | 2,000–3,500 THB/month |
| SIM / data | ~300 THB/month | ~500 THB/month |

On transport: a scooter is the standard way to get around near Chiang Mai camps. Grab exists in the city but adds up fast if you rely on it daily. Most long-stay trainees rent a scooter within their first week. See [scooter rental in Chiang Mai](/stay-train/scooter-rental) for what to expect at camp.

## The DTV visa: what it costs and how it changes the math

For anyone planning more than three months in Thailand, the **Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)** changes the financial picture.

Here are the core numbers:

- **DTV visa fee**: 10,000 THB, paid at a Thai embassy or consulate abroad (single application)
- **Proof of funds**: 500,000 THB visible in your bank account at the time of application. This money stays in your account; you do not spend it.
- **Validity**: 5 years, multi-entry, 180 days per stay
- **Qualifying activity for Muay Thai**: you need to be enrolled in a Muay Thai training programme. The embassy wants documentation from the camp confirming your enrolment.

At Wildcat, the enrolment letter and training documentation required for a [DTV visa for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) is bundled with the monthly training package. There is no hidden DTV surcharge. We have been supporting trainees through this process for years.

### DTV vs ED visa: the real cost comparison

The **ED (Education) visa** is the older alternative. It sounds cheaper upfront but carries recurring costs:

- Requires course renewal approximately 4 times per year
- Each renewal involves a trip to immigration, fees, and in some cases new course documentation
- Reporting obligations every 90 days add further time and cost

Over a full year, the **DTV is typically cheaper and simpler** for serious long-stayers. The 500,000 THB proof-of-funds sounds large, but it stays in your account. Your actual cash outlay is your monthly living and training cost, not that reserve.

For the full picture on applying, see [how to apply for the DTV visa](/dtv-visa/how-to-apply) and the [long-stay training on a DTV](/dtv-visa/long-stay-training) guide.

## How to budget your first month: a practical checklist

If this is your first training trip to Thailand, here is the order of operations:

1. Decide your tier: budget (~24,000 THB/month) or comfortable (~37,500 THB/month). Be honest with yourself. Training twice a day is tiring, and comfort helps recovery.
2. Book your training package first. Most camps require prepayment for monthly packages. Lock in the camp before finding accommodation.
3. Find accommodation within 10–15 minutes of your camp. Use Google Maps and filter by distance to the gym.
4. Budget 6,000–10,000 THB for food. Eat street food regularly, cook occasionally if your place has a kitchen. Markets near Hang Dong are excellent.
5. Rent a scooter on arrival. The going rate in Chiang Mai is around 3,000 THB per month for a reliable automatic. Do not rent from the airport.
6. Hold a buffer. Keep at least 5,000 THB in reserve for the first month; unforeseen costs (medical kit, equipment, a day trip) always come up.

Total first-month realistic spend at the comfortable tier: ~42,000–45,000 THB, including the buffer and any gear you pick up locally.

## FAQ on Muay Thai training costs in Thailand

### How much does it cost to train Muay Thai in Thailand per month?

A monthly unlimited training package in Thailand costs between 8,000 and 25,000 THB depending on the camp and location. In Chiang Mai, budget camps run 8,000–12,000 THB per month. Add accommodation, food, and transport, and total monthly costs start around 24,000 THB on a budget and 37,500 THB at a comfortable level.

### Is it cheaper to train Muay Thai in Thailand?

Yes, significantly. Thailand has the highest-quality Muay Thai coaching in the world at costs far lower than equivalent fitness programmes in Europe, the US, or Australia. A full month of twice-daily training with Thai coaches in Chiang Mai costs less than a typical monthly gym membership in London.

### How much money do you need to live and train Muay Thai in Thailand?

Budget a minimum of 24,000 THB (~$670) per month for a no-frills training lifestyle in Chiang Mai. For a more comfortable standard (private condo, decent food, a scooter), plan for 37,500 THB (~$1,040) per month. These figures are for Chiang Mai; Phuket and Bangkok run 30–50% higher for the same quality of life.

### Is Chiang Mai or Phuket cheaper for Muay Thai training?

Chiang Mai is consistently 30–40% cheaper than Phuket for a comparable training lifestyle. Training fees, accommodation, and food all cost less in Chiang Mai. The training quality at established Chiang Mai camps is equal to Phuket's major operations; the cost gap is purely geographic. See the full comparison in [Chiang Mai vs Phuket for Muay Thai training](/chiang-mai-vs-phuket-muay-thai).

### What is the cost of a Muay Thai course for a DTV visa?

There is no fixed "DTV course fee" set by the Thai government. The requirement is enrolment in a legitimate Muay Thai training programme, so the cost is your regular monthly training package. At Wildcat, training documentation for the DTV application is included with standard enrolment. The visa itself costs 10,000 THB, paid at the Thai embassy.

### How much is the DTV visa for Muay Thai?

The DTV visa fee is **10,000 THB**, paid at a Thai embassy or consulate when you apply. You also need to show 500,000 THB in your bank account (proof of funds). That amount stays in your account; it is not a fee. The visa is valid for 5 years with entries of up to 180 days each.

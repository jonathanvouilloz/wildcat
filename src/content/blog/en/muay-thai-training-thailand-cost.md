## What Muay Thai training actually costs

Training fees are the core of your budget. Here's the real range across Thailand.

### Per session (drop-in)

The **per-session rate** in Thailand ranges from **300 to 600 THB**. Northern Thailand camps, including those in the Chiang Mai and Hang Dong area, typically sit at the lower end of that range. Phuket camps, particularly the large international operations near Chalong, charge closer to 550–800 THB per session (~$16–23 USD).

The [full rate card](/classes#pricing), kids' prices included, is always the reference if anything changes.

Practically: if you're planning a short visit of one to two weeks, drop-in is fine. For a month or longer, a monthly package is far better value.

### Monthly unlimited training

Most camps offer unlimited training packages. Here's the market range as of 2026:

- **Budget / northern Thailand camps**: 8,000–12,000 THB per month
- **Mid-range camps in Phuket or Bangkok**: 12,000–18,000 THB per month
- **Premium all-inclusive operations** (Tiger Muay Thai, Fairtex Bangkok): 15,000–25,000 THB per month for training alone

Our 5,000 THB month sits below the usual northern range. We are a small family camp in Hang Dong, outside the city centre, and we price like one.

Worth knowing: **most camps offer a 10–20% discount** for commitments of three months or longer. If you're planning a serious training block, ask about long-stay rates when you enquire. It's standard practice and camps expect the question.

### What monthly training typically includes

A standard monthly package at a Thai camp covers:

- Two sessions per day (morning and afternoon)
- Padwork with a trainer in most sessions
- Bag rounds, clinch drilling, and technique work
- Group sparring (varies by camp culture)

What is usually charged separately: private one-on-one sessions, strength and conditioning classes, MMA or BJJ cross-training access, and any gym facilities beyond the Muay Thai area. Factor these in if they're part of your plan.

## Accommodation near a Muay Thai camp

Rent is the second-largest cost. Location matters: suburban camps like those in **Hang Dong** (south of Chiang Mai city) are in a lower-rent zone than the trendy Nimman area. That difference is real, sometimes 3,000–5,000 THB per month for a comparable room.

### Budget accommodation (Chiang Mai area)

- Studio room or shared house near a suburban camp: **4,000–7,000 THB per month**
- Shared facilities, basic furnishings, adequate for a focused training trip
- Many fighters and long-stay trainees choose this tier to stretch their training budget

### Comfortable accommodation (1-bedroom condo, private)

- Hang Dong / Chiang Mai suburbs: **7,000–12,000 THB per month**
- Nimman city centre: 12,000–20,000 THB per month
- Phuket (near Tiger Muay Thai / Sinbi): 10,000–18,000 THB per month
- Bangkok (near Fairtex): 12,000–22,000 THB per month

If you would rather not look for a room at all, our Stay & Train offer is 28,000 THB per person per month, or 8,000 THB a week for a shorter trip. It includes a private room in a shared house with all bills paid, unlimited training, a motorbike and the airport pickup. Meals and petrol are not included. The [Stay & Train page](/stay-train#packages) lists exactly what is in and out.

A practical tip: the 10–15 minute rule. If your accommodation is more than 15 minutes from your camp by scooter, you will lose training time and motivation over a long stay. Proximity to the gym matters more than neighbourhood.

## Food and daily expenses

Chiang Mai is one of the most affordable cities in Southeast Asia for food. Street market meals cost 60–80 THB. Sit-down restaurants for foreigners run 150–300 THB per meal. If you eat street food most of the time (which most long-stay trainees do), your food costs stay low.

A realistic breakdown of **daily living expenses in Chiang Mai** as of 2026:

| Item | Low budget | Comfortable |
|---|---|---|
| Street food / market meals | 60–80 THB/meal | 80–150 THB/meal |
| Monthly food estimate | 6,000–8,000 THB | 9,000–14,000 THB |

Most long-stay trainees rent a scooter within their first week. We rent Honda Scoopys at the camp for 350 THB a day or 3,000 THB a month. ## Visa costs in 2026

Thailand changed its entry rules in September 2026, so older cost guides are now wrong on the visa line. Here is what you pay today, by route.

| Route | Stay | Government cost |
|---|---|---|
| Visa exemption (most Western passports) | 30 days on arrival | Free |
| Exemption plus one extension | Up to 60 days | 1,900 THB, at the officer's discretion |
| Tourist visa (TR), applied for before you fly | 60 days, plus one 30-day extension | Embassy fee (varies by country), plus 1,900 THB for the extension |
| DTV (Destination Thailand Visa) | 180 days per entry, valid 5 years | 10,000 THB, plus 1,900 THB if you extend a stay by 180 days |

A few details matter for your budget:

- **The 30-day exemption applies since 15 September 2026**, down from 60 days, for 60 countries and territories including the UK, the US, Canada, Australia and the EU, [according to the Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tatnews.org/2026/09/thailand-introduces-new-30-day-and-15-day-visa-exemption-rules-from-15-september/). The one 30-day extension costs 1,900 THB with the TM.7 form, and approval is not automatic, [as Thai embassies confirmed on 17 September](https://visasnews.com/en/thailand-30-day-visa-free-stay-can-be-extended-by-another-30-days/).
- **The TR still gives 60 days plus 30**, and the in-country extension is the same 1,900 THB, [per ISSA Compass](https://www.issacompass.com/insights/thailand-tourist-visa-vs-visa-exemption-in-2026-what-the-60-day-stay-rule-actual). The visa fee itself depends on the embassy you apply to.
- **The DTV fee is 10,000 THB**, non-refundable, as the [Royal Thai Embassy in Vientiane](https://vientiane.thaiembassy.org/en/page/destination-thailand-visa-dtv) lists it. Since 31 August 2026 you also need a police certificate, which has its own fee in your home country. [What changed on 31 August](/blog/dtv-visa-new-requirements-2026) covers it.
- **A tourist entry fee is coming, but not yet.** On 14 August 2026 the National Tourism Policy Committee approved in principle a fee of 450 THB per foreign visitor, [reported The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/tourism/40069809). It still needs a public hearing and Cabinet approval. Collection is targeted for the first quarter of 2027, on air arrivals first. Today you pay nothing, and there is no legal start date.
- **The TDAC arrival card is free.** Fill it in on [tdac.immigration.go.th](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/) before each entry and ignore sites that charge for it.

### The DTV route in one paragraph

For six months or more, the DTV is usually the route that fits. The 500,000 THB proof of funds has to sit in your account, but you do not spend it. What you do spend is the 10,000 THB fee and the training programme the visa is based on, since embassies in practice want to see at least six months of training. At Wildcat we prepare the enrolment letter and programme description at no extra charge: you pay for the training, nothing more. For the full DTV bill, course, fee and the optional agent, see [the real cost of the DTV visa](/blog/dtv-visa-cost-breakdown). This article stays on the cost of living and training around it.

The **ED (Education) visa** is the older alternative. For non-formal schools such as gyms, it works through extensions in roughly 90-day blocks, and each block means another trip to immigration and another fee. Over a year, the DTV is usually simpler. [ED visa vs DTV for Muay Thai](/blog/ed-visa-vs-dtv-muay-thai) compares the two in detail.

*Visa rules and fees change. Check current requirements with your nearest Thai embassy before applying.*

## How to budget your first month: a practical checklist

If this is your first training trip to Thailand, here is the order of operations:

1. Decide your tier: budget (~24,000 THB/month) or comfortable (~37,500 THB/month). Be honest with yourself. Training twice a day is tiring, and comfort helps recovery.
2. Book your training package first. Most camps require prepayment for monthly packages. Lock in the camp before finding accommodation.
3. Find accommodation within 10–15 minutes of your camp. Use Google Maps and filter by distance to the gym.
4. Budget 6,000–10,000 THB for food. Eat street food regularly, cook occasionally if your place has a kitchen. Markets near Hang Dong are excellent.
5. Rent a scooter on arrival. The going rate in Chiang Mai is around 3,000 THB per month for a reliable automatic, which is also what we charge at the camp. Do not rent from the airport.
6. Hold a buffer. Keep at least 5,000 THB in reserve for the first month; unforeseen costs (medical kit, equipment, a day trip) always come up.

Total first-month realistic spend at the comfortable tier: ~42,000–45,000 THB, including the buffer and any gear you pick up locally.

## FAQ on Muay Thai training costs in Thailand

### How much does it cost to train Muay Thai in Thailand per month?

A monthly unlimited training package in Thailand costs between 8,000 and 25,000 THB depending on the camp and location. In Chiang Mai, budget camps run 8,000–12,000 THB per month. At Wildcat, unlimited training is 5,000 THB a month. Add accommodation, food, and transport, and total monthly costs start around 24,000 THB on a budget and 37,500 THB at a comfortable level.

### How much is a month at Wildcat with accommodation?

Our Stay & Train month is 28,000 THB per person. It covers a private room in a shared house with bills, unlimited training, a motorbike and the airport pickup. Budget roughly 9,000 THB more for food, petrol and a SIM, so about 36,900 THB in total.

### Do I have to pay a tourist tax to enter Thailand?

Not today. A 450 THB entry fee was approved in principle on 14 August 2026, with collection targeted for the first quarter of 2027 on air arrivals. It still needs Cabinet approval, so nothing is collected yet.

### Is it cheaper to train Muay Thai in Thailand?

Yes, significantly. Thailand has the highest-quality Muay Thai coaching in the world at costs far lower than equivalent fitness programmes in Europe, the US, or Australia. A full month of twice-daily training with Thai coaches in Chiang Mai costs less than a typical monthly gym membership in London.

### How much money do you need to live and train Muay Thai in Thailand?

Budget a minimum of 24,000 THB (~$670) per month for a no-frills training lifestyle in Chiang Mai. For a more comfortable standard (private condo, decent food, a scooter), plan for 37,500 THB (~$1,040) per month. These figures are for Chiang Mai; Phuket and Bangkok run 30–50% higher for the same quality of life.

### Is Chiang Mai or Phuket cheaper for Muay Thai training?

Chiang Mai is consistently 30–40% cheaper than Phuket for a comparable training lifestyle. Training fees, accommodation, and food all cost less in Chiang Mai. The training quality at established Chiang Mai camps is equal to Phuket's major operations; the cost gap is purely geographic. See the full comparison in [Chiang Mai vs Phuket for Muay Thai training](/blog/chiang-mai-vs-phuket-muay-thai).

### What is the cost of a Muay Thai course for a DTV visa?

There is no fixed "DTV course fee" set by the Thai government. The requirement is enrolment in a legitimate Muay Thai training programme, so the cost is your regular monthly training package. At Wildcat, training documentation for the DTV application is included with enrolment, at no extra charge. The visa itself costs 10,000 THB. The full DTV bill is broken down in [the real cost of the DTV visa](/blog/dtv-visa-cost-breakdown).

### How much is the DTV visa for Muay Thai?

The DTV visa fee is **10,000 THB**, paid at a Thai embassy or consulate when you apply. You also need to show 500,000 THB in your bank account (proof of funds). That amount stays in your account; it is not a fee. The visa is valid for 5 years with entries of up to 180 days each.

It depends entirely on how long you plan to stay. For a short trip, a tourist visa with repeated border runs can still work out cheaper than **the DTV**. For anything longer than a few months, the math flips fast.

One applicant on an online forum calculated that six months of tourist visa runs came to around **6,800 THB** total, against 10,000 THB for the DTV, a gap of roughly 3,200 THB. Call that a convenience tax for not having to leave the country every couple of months. It is a real example, not a universal rule; flights, routes, and nationality all change the number. This article works out where the DTV or repeated border runs actually cost less, scenario by scenario, so you can run the numbers for your own plan.

## What a border run actually involves

A **Thailand border run** means leaving the country and coming back in on a new tourist entry, usually to reset the clock on how long you can legally stay. It is not a special visa category of its own. It is a workaround built on top of ordinary tourist entry rules.

The standard tourist visa allows 60 days, with one 30-day extension available in-country, for a maximum of **90 days** per cycle. Once that runs out, the only way to keep staying is to leave Thailand and re-enter on a fresh tourist entry, which is the border run itself. Some travellers do this by air to a neighbouring country, others overland to Laos, Cambodia, or Myanmar and back the same day.

**How many border runs are realistic per year** depends on how long each cycle lasts and how quickly you turn each one around. At roughly 90 days per cycle, a full year could mean three to four repeats, each with its own transport cost and a day or two out of your schedule. Several sources covering Thai immigration in 2025 and 2026 describe closer scrutiny of travellers who cross back and forth on tourist entries very frequently, though there is no single confirmed figure for how many runs immigration officers will tolerate before asking questions. If frequent border running is part of your plan, it is worth checking the current stance with your point of entry before relying on it long-term.

This article stays on the cost question. If you are weighing whether a border run routine even fits your training plan in the first place, [the DTV visa for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) lays out how the three visa options compare for that purpose.

## The DTV's own costs, including the optional extension

The DTV itself has a fixed, simple cost structure, and that is the whole appeal against something as variable as repeated travel. The embassy application fee is a flat **10,000 THB**, paid once, and it is non-refundable whether you are approved or not, according to [The Thaiger's DTV guide](https://thethaiger.com/guides/visa-information/thailand-digital-nomad-visa).

Once you are in Thailand on a DTV, each entry gives you 180 days, and you can extend that same entry once for another 180 days, for a total of up to 360 days continuous per entry, at a cost of **1,900 THB**. That extension option resets with every new entry, but it is not something you pay repeatedly within a single stay the way a border run cycle repeats. You apply for the DTV itself through the [official Thailand e-Visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th), the only system the government runs for this visa, and only from outside Thailand.

## Adding up the real cost of repeated border runs

The honest total for repeated border runs is never just the visa fee. It is the visa or extension cost, plus the transport to actually leave and come back, every single cycle.

Break it into three pieces:

- **The entry or extension itself.** A tourist visa entry or the in-country 30-day extension typically runs in a modest range, often cited somewhere around 1,900 to 2,000 THB for the extension, broadly similar to the DTV's own extension fee, though this figure is worth confirming against a current, official source rather than treated as fixed.
- **Transport for the run.** This is where the real variation lives. Flight costs for repeated visa runs can range from a few hundred THB for a short overland hop to a couple of thousand THB for a flight to a neighbouring country, depending on the route, the season, and how far ahead you book. There is no single number here, and anyone who quotes you one flat flight cost for every route is guessing.
- **Your own time.** A day or two lost to travel every cycle, which does not show up on a receipt but is a real cost for anyone working or training on a schedule.

Reworking the reported 6,800 THB for six months example above, that figure already bundled one or two cycles of extension and transport together. Stretch the same pattern to a full year, doubling the cycles, and the total for repeated border runs climbs toward somewhere in the **13,000 to 15,000 THB** range, transport included, well past the DTV's flat 10,000 THB fee. The exact number for you depends entirely on your own route and how often you actually leave.

## Where the breakeven point actually is

For a stay under roughly three to six months, border runs frequently still come out cheaper in raw cost, since you avoid the DTV fee entirely and only need one or two cycles. Past that point, the math shifts toward the DTV for most travellers.

## FAQ on the DTV vs tourist visa runs cost

### What is a border run in Thailand?

A border run is leaving Thailand and re-entering on a new tourist entry to reset your allowed stay, once your existing tourist visa and any extension have run out. It is not its own visa category, just a way of restarting the standard tourist entry rules.

### How many border runs can you realistically do in a year?

At roughly 90 days per cycle (60-day entry plus a 30-day extension), three to four cycles a year is typical for someone relying on border runs continuously. Some sources describe closer scrutiny of very frequent crossings in 2025 and 2026, though there is no single confirmed cap on how many runs are tolerated, so checking current practice at your entry point is worth doing if this is a long-term plan.

### Is the DTV visa cheaper than doing repeated border runs?

Usually, yes, once a stay passes roughly six months to a year. Under that, a tourist visa with border runs can still be cheaper in raw cost, since it avoids the DTV's 10,000 THB fee outright. Past that point, the flights and repeated extensions of border running tend to add up past what the DTV costs.

### What's the difference between a border run and a visa run?

In practice, the terms describe the same thing: leaving Thailand and re-entering to reset a tourist stay. Some people use "visa run" more loosely to include getting a new visa at a consulate abroad rather than a same-day border crossing, but both aim at the same outcome.

### Does the DTV visa extension cost the same as a tourist visa extension?

The DTV's in-country extension is a confirmed 1,900 THB. A standard tourist visa extension is often cited in a similar range, but that figure is worth confirming directly rather than assumed identical, since extension fees and rules can be updated by Thai immigration.

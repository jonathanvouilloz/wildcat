It depends on how long you plan to stay, and since **15 September 2026** the answer has moved. For a short trip, the visa exemption or a tourist visa is still cheaper than **the DTV**. For anything past a few months, the new 30-day rule makes repeated visa runs add up faster than they used to.

Before the change, one applicant on an online forum calculated that six months of tourist visa runs came to around **6,800 THB** total, against 10,000 THB for the DTV. That example was built on 60-day visa-free entries. With entries now halved to 30 days, the same six months takes more extensions and more exits. This article reworks the numbers, scenario by scenario, so you can run them for your own plan.

*Updated 14 September 2026 for the 30-day visa exemption and the DTV filing rules that changed on 31 August.*

## What a border run involves after 15 September 2026

A **Thailand border run** means leaving the country and coming back in on a new entry, usually to reset the clock on how long you can legally stay. It is not a visa category of its own. It is a workaround built on top of ordinary tourist entry rules, and there are now two sets of rules to build it on.

**The visa exemption.** Travellers from the 60 countries and territories on the list, including the UK, the US, most of Europe, Canada and Australia, get **30 days** on arrival from 15 September 2026, according to the [Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tatnews.org/2026/09/thailand-introduces-new-30-day-and-15-day-visa-exemption-rules-from-15-september/). You can ask an immigration office for one extension of up to 30 days, for **1,900 THB**, so a single visa-exempt cycle lasts 60 days at most. Entries on the exemption through land border checkpoints are capped at **two per calendar year**, so the cheap overland hop to Laos or Cambodia only works twice.

**The tourist visa (TR).** Unchanged: 60 days, extendable once by 30, for **90 days** per cycle, [per ISSA Compass](https://www.issacompass.com/insights/thailand-tourist-visa-vs-visa-exemption-in-2026-what-the-60-day-stay-rule-actual). The catch is that you apply for it through the e-Visa system before you travel, and a single-entry TR is used up when you leave. Relying on it for back-to-back stays means a new application, and a new government fee that varies by embassy, every cycle.

**How many visa runs are realistic per year** now depends on which route you use. On the exemption alone, a full year means around six 60-day cycles, five exits and six extensions, with no more than two of those exits over land. Immigration officers also assess each visa-exempt arrival at their discretion, and repeated back-to-back entries are exactly the pattern that draws questions. There is no published number of runs they will tolerate, so if frequent border running is your plan, expect it to get harder the more often you do it.

This article stays on the cost question. If you are weighing whether a visa run routine even fits your training plan, [the DTV visa for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) lays out how the three visa options compare for that purpose.

## The DTV's own costs, including the optional extension

The DTV has a fixed, simple cost structure, and that is the whole appeal against something as variable as repeated travel. The embassy application fee is a flat **10,000 THB**, paid once, and it is non-refundable whether you are approved or not, according to [The Thaiger's DTV guide](https://thethaiger.com/guides/visa-information/thailand-digital-nomad-visa).

Once you are in Thailand on a DTV, each entry gives you 180 days, and you can extend that same entry once for another 180 days, for up to 360 days continuous per entry, at a cost of **1,900 THB**. That extension option resets with every new entry, but you do not pay it repeatedly within a single stay the way a visa run cycle repeats. You apply for the DTV through the [official Thailand e-Visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th), from outside Thailand, and since 31 August 2026 only with the Thai mission covering your country of nationality or legal residence. [The new DTV rules](/blog/dtv-visa-new-requirements-2026) explain what that changes, including the criminal record certificate now required.

The new 30-day rule does not touch DTV holders. If you enter on a valid DTV, you still get 180 days per entry.

## Adding up the real cost of repeated visa runs

The honest total for repeated visa runs is never just the extension fee. It is the extension or visa cost, plus the transport to actually leave and come back, every single cycle.

Break it into three pieces:

- **The extension or visa itself.** The in-country extension of a visa-exempt entry costs 1,900 THB, the same as the DTV's own extension. A TR visa adds its own application fee each cycle, set in local currency by each embassy.
- **Transport for the run.** This is where the real variation lives. A short overland hop can cost a few hundred THB, and a flight to a neighbouring country a couple of thousand, depending on the route, the season and how far ahead you book. With land entries on the exemption capped at two a year, most runs in a long stay become flights. Anyone who quotes you one flat transport cost for every route is guessing.
- **Your own time.** A day or two lost to travel every cycle, which does not show up on a receipt but is a real cost for anyone working or training on a schedule.

![Suitcase and boarding pass laid out ahead of a Thailand visa border run](/images/blog/dtv-vs-tourist-visa-runs-cost-2.webp)

Take six months on the exemption alone. That is three 60-day cycles, so three extensions at 1,900 THB (5,700 THB) and two exits. Add a few hundred to a few thousand THB per exit and the total lands somewhere around **7,000 to 12,000 THB**, already level with the DTV's flat 10,000 THB fee. The old 6,800 THB example sits below that range because it was built on 60-day entries.

## Where the breakeven point is now

For a stay under about three months, the exemption or a TR visa still comes out cheaper in raw cost, since you avoid the DTV fee entirely. Between four and six months the two routes cost about the same, and past six months the math shifts toward the DTV for most travellers.

| Length of stay | Visa exemption and runs (illustrative) | DTV visa | Cheaper option |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 month | 30-day entry, nothing to pay in Thailand | 10,000 THB flat | Exemption, clearly |
| 2 months | 30-day entry plus one 1,900 THB extension | 10,000 THB flat | Exemption |
| 3 months | One extension and one exit, roughly 2,500 to 5,000 THB, or a TR visa applied for before you fly | 10,000 THB flat | Tourist route, usually |
| 6 months | 3 extensions and 2 exits, roughly 7,000 to 12,000 THB | 10,000 THB flat | Close call |
| 1 year | 6 extensions and 5 exits, no more than 2 over land, roughly 17,000 to 25,000 THB | 10,000 THB, plus 1,900 THB if you extend | DTV |
| 2 years | Roughly 35,000 to 50,000 THB, with growing scrutiny at entry | 10,000 THB plus extensions, around 12,000 to 14,000 THB total | DTV, clearly |
| 5 years | Not a realistic plan on repeated visa-exempt entries | 10,000 THB plus extensions across re-entries, likely 25,000 to 30,000 THB total | DTV, by a wide margin |

These figures are illustrative, built from the official extension fee and a range of transport costs, not a precise formula. Plug in your own flights to see where your **breakeven point** sits.

Cost aside, the DTV also removes the logistics: no repeated exits, no day lost to travel every month or two, no officer deciding at the counter whether your fifth entry of the year looks like tourism, and a visa that stays valid for **5 years**. ## FAQ on the DTV vs tourist visa runs cost

### What is a border run in Thailand?

A border run is leaving Thailand and re-entering to reset your allowed stay, once your current entry and any extension have run out. It is not its own visa category, just a way of restarting the standard tourist entry rules.

### Can you still do visa runs after 15 September 2026?

Yes, but each visa-exempt entry now gives 30 days, extendable once by 30 days for 1,900 THB, and only two entries a year on the exemption can be made through land border checkpoints. Beyond that, runs mean flying, and officers keep discretion over repeated visa-exempt arrivals.

### How many visa runs can you realistically do in a year?

On the visa exemption alone, around five exits and six extensions to cover a full year, since each cycle lasts 60 days at most. With a TR visa each cycle lasts up to 90 days, but you need a new visa for each one. There is no published cap on air entries, so how many runs are tolerated in practice comes down to the officer at your point of entry.

### Is the DTV visa cheaper than doing repeated visa runs?

Usually, yes, once a stay passes four to six months. Under that, the exemption or a TR visa can still be cheaper in raw cost, since it avoids the DTV's 10,000 THB fee outright. Past six months, extensions and flights add up past what the DTV costs.

### What's the difference between a border run and a visa run?

In practice, the terms describe the same thing: leaving Thailand and re-entering to reset a tourist stay. Some people use "visa run" more loosely to include getting a new visa at a consulate abroad rather than a same-day border crossing, but both aim at the same outcome.

### Does the DTV visa extension cost the same as a tourist extension?

Yes. The DTV's in-country extension and the 30-day extension of a visa-exempt entry both cost 1,900 THB. The difference is what you get: 180 more days on a DTV, 30 on the exemption.

Under about three months, the visa exemption or a TR visa is still the cheaper option. It also comes with a visa that stays valid for 5 years instead of a routine you have to repeat every couple of months.

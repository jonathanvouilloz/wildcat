Structure or flexibility. That's what the choice between the **Muay Thai education visa** and the DTV comes down to once you look past the paperwork. The education visa ties you to one licensed school with real attendance rules. The DTV doesn't tie you to a school, but it asks for a different kind of discipline in how you build your file. If you're deciding between the two to train Muay Thai in Thailand, here's how each one works in practice, what it costs, and who it suits.

*Updated 16 October 2026: this guide now covers how the Muay Thai ED visa actually works (school licensing, 90-day extensions, fees, attendance), what changes in Chiang Mai, and the DTV rules in force since 31 August 2026.*

I'm Meaw. I own and run Wildcat Muay Thai in Chiang Mai, and I write the soft power enrolment documents myself for trainees who apply for the DTV through us. Wildcat is not an ED school, so I don't sponsor education visas, but I've sat across the table from enough students choosing between the two to know where people usually get it wrong. This isn't a sales pitch for either visa, just the comparison I wish existed before I started fielding the same three questions every month.

## What is the Muay Thai education visa?

**The Muay Thai education visa is a Non-Immigrant ED visa sponsored by a Muay Thai gym that is licensed as a private school.** Under Thai law, a Muay Thai school sits in the same family as language, cooking and massage schools: a private non-formal school under the Private School Act B.E. 2550, overseen by the Office of the Private Education Commission (OPEC). The extension rules for these schools are set by Immigration Bureau Order No. 12/2568 of 23 January 2025, clause 2.9, [according to Thai Law Online's 2026 review of the order](https://www.thailawonline.com/student-visa-in-thailand/).

That licence is the whole point. A gym that simply trains foreigners can't sponsor you, however good its coaching. Thai embassies ask for a letter from the school plus a certificate from the education authority, and the [Royal Thai Embassy in Phnom Penh](https://phnompenh.thaiembassy.org/en/publicservice/non-immigrant-ed-visa) names the Office of the Private Education Commission as one of those authorities for private schools. If a gym offers you an "ED visa" and cannot show you its private school licence, walk away.

The visa itself is short on paper. The embassy issues it with 3 months of validity and a stay of up to 90 days, and Thai Immigration can then extend it for up to one year from your first entry, per the same Phnom Penh embassy page.

## How long can you stay on a Muay Thai ED visa?

**Up to one year per entry, in blocks of 90 days.** Each extension runs for up to 90 days, for the period your school certifies, and officers may grant less. The one-year ceiling counts from your date of entry, or from the date an in-country change of visa type was approved, [per Thai Law Online](https://www.thailawonline.com/student-visa-in-thailand/).

In practice that means four 90-day blocks: the first one on the visa, then three extensions at your local immigration office. Each renewal depends on the school confirming you're still enrolled and attending. Staying longer than that year means starting again with a fresh application, not adding a fifth extension.

Leaving Thailand mid-block needs planning too. Without a re-entry permit, your permission to stay ends the moment you leave. A single re-entry permit costs 1,000 THB and a multiple one 3,800 THB, and you must also file a free 90-day address report while you're in the country.

## How do you get a Muay Thai ED visa, step by step?

1. **Choose a licensed school.** Ask for its private school licence and how it handles immigration paperwork before you pay anything.
2. **Enrol and pay the course.** The school then requests the education authority letter. One Chiang Mai guide puts that approval at 6 to 8 weeks, and 8 to 12 weeks from first contact to visa, [according to CMLocals](https://cmlocals.com/ed-visa/muay-thai/) (last verified March 2026).
3. **Apply for the visa.** From outside Thailand, you file on the [official e-Visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th) or at an embassy, with processing usually taking 3 to 10 working days according to Thai Law Online. Some people convert in-country at an immigration office instead, which requires at least 15 days left on your current permission, per Thai Law Online. CMLocals reports that this changeover is rarely possible in Chiang Mai, so plan on applying from abroad.
4. **Extend every 90 days.** Each time you bring the 1,900 THB fee and the school's paperwork to immigration.
5. **Keep your reporting and re-entry permits in order** for as long as you hold the visa.

Since 15 September 2026, most Western passports get 30 days on arrival instead of 60, [per the Tourism Authority of Thailand](https://www.tatnews.org/2026/09/thailand-introduces-new-30-day-and-15-day-visa-exemption-rules-from-15-september/). With the 15-day rule above, that leaves very little room to convert after you land. Treat in-country conversion as the exception.

## What does a Muay Thai education visa cost?

Government fees are the small part. [Thai Law Online lists](https://www.thailawonline.com/student-visa-in-thailand/) a 2,000 THB visa or conversion fee, 1,900 THB per extension, 1,000 or 3,800 THB for re-entry permits, and up to 5,000 THB in fines for a late 90-day report. Embassies abroad charge their own equivalent: the Phnom Penh post lists 80 USD for a single-entry ED.

The school is the big part. CMLocals publishes one Chiang Mai Muay Thai ED programme at **33,900 to 83,700 THB** for 6 to 12 months, depending on the number of classes, with enrolment, immigration processing and extension fees included. For non-formal schools in general, Thai Law Online estimates 45,000 to 60,000 THB a year including government fees. Prices vary a lot between schools, so ask each one exactly what its package includes.

For comparison, the DTV costs a 10,000 THB visa fee, plus the training itself. At Wildcat, unlimited group training is 5,000 THB a month and the enrolment letter costs nothing extra. Itemised numbers for every layer are in [the full DTV visa cost breakdown](/blog/dtv-visa-cost-breakdown).

## Attendance: what immigration actually checks

The education visa is a study visa, and immigration treats it that way. For non-formal schools, every extension application must include the course outline, the timetable and the hours you have attended so far, per clause 2.9 of the 2025 order as summarised by [Thai Law Online](https://www.thailawonline.com/student-visa-in-thailand/). The same review notes that the widely repeated "80% attendance" rule does not appear in any official instrument it read. Any threshold comes from the school's own rules, which is exactly why a school with weak records is a risk for you.

CMLocals adds a practical warning from Chiang Mai: gaps of more than 30 days are a problem, and travel is generally discouraged because immigration expects continuous attendance. If your plan includes a month in Bali halfway through, the ED is a poor fit.

## The Muay Thai ED visa in Chiang Mai

Chiang Mai is one of the main places people look for a Muay Thai education visa, for obvious reasons: lower living costs than Bangkok or Phuket, plenty of gyms, and a big long-stay community. A few things are specific to the city:

- **Your extensions and 90-day reports happen at Chiang Mai Immigration**, not in Bangkok, so you deal with the same office for the whole year.
- **Not every gym holds a school licence.** Many Chiang Mai camps, Wildcat included, are training camps, not licensed private schools, so your ED shortlist will be shorter than your list of good gyms.
- **Distance matters for attendance.** If your ED school is across town from where you live, the attendance logs suffer before your training does. Choose the school first, then the flat.
- **The DTV route is wider.** The DTV doesn't need a licensed school, only a genuine training programme, so you can pick a gym for its coaching and atmosphere rather than for its licence.

Wildcat sits in Hang Dong, about 15 minutes south of the city. If you go the DTV route with us, you train at an open-air camp and can [plan accommodation and training together](/stay-train).

## What the DTV offers instead

The **DTV** works on a completely different structure. In [The Thaiger's own summary](https://thethaiger.com/guides/visa-information/thailand-digital-nomad-visa) of the core specs: "five-year validity, multiple entry, 180 days per entry, extendable once per entry for an additional 180 days." There's no school to answer to and no attendance record to maintain. Train at one camp for a month, move to another, fly home, come back, all without renewing an enrollment.

Remote work is part of that same flexibility. The DTV lets you keep working for a foreign employer while you're in Thailand, something the ED was never built for since it exists to support study, not income. If your plan involves training in the morning and logging into a job in another country in the afternoon, that alone often settles the decision.

The DTV asks for more upfront, though. You need around 500,000 THB in your own account, and since 31 August 2026 you must file at the Thai mission covering your country of nationality or legal residence, with a criminal record certificate. [Our article on the new DTV requirements](/blog/dtv-visa-new-requirements-2026) covers that change in detail, and [the full DTV visa guide](/dtv-visa) covers eligibility end to end.

## Where Muay Thai actually fits in this decision

Muay Thai can technically justify either route, which is exactly why the choice gets confusing. Licensed schools sponsor students on the ED for full-time Muay Thai programmes. The DTV recognizes Muay Thai as a soft power activity in its own right. On paper, both work.

In practice, they ask for a different commitment. The ED route means immersion at one school, your visa status depending on that relationship holding up. The DTV route stays flexible: train at Wildcat for a stretch, try another camp, take time off, or split your stay between training and remote work, without any of it touching your visa status.

Worth being precise here: **an education visa in Thailand built around Muay Thai** is not the same case as language schools. As [The Thaiger reports](https://thethaiger.com/guides/visa-information/thailand-digital-nomad-visa), citing the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, "Thai language schools were removed from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' qualifying soft power list" in 2025, and applicants wanting to study Thai are now directed to the ED visa instead. Muay Thai wasn't affected and remains valid for the DTV. If you see older content suggesting language study still qualifies for the DTV, treat it as outdated. For the comparison across all three visa types, [the DTV vs ED vs tourist comparison table](/dtv-visa#compare) breaks down eligibility, cost and administration side by side.

## What Meaw sees on real DTV files

I can't promise anyone a visa approval, and I wouldn't trust anyone who does. What I can tell you is what I see, month after month, when I help put a DTV soft power file together for Muay Thai.

Consistency stands out most. Immigration officers aren't looking at one document in isolation, they're checking whether the enrollment letter, the training schedule, and the applicant's own account of their plan tell the same story. A file where the dates don't quite line up, or the training description reads generic and copy-pasted, lands differently than one that matches a real person's actual plan.

Documentation quality matters more than people expect too. A one-line letter saying "this person trains here" carries less weight than one that describes the programme, the hours, and what the training involves. That part is on the gym to get right, not the applicant. The same logic applies to the ED, only there it's tested every 90 days.

None of this means the DTV suits everyone. If you can't commit to a genuine, sustained training plan, no amount of paperwork changes that. This route rewards real plans, not polished paperwork.

## How to decide

Worth saying plainly: **Thailand's visa rules keep evolving.** The DTV dropped general language schools from its soft power list in 2025 and added a home-country filing rule and a criminal record certificate on 31 August 2026. The visa exemption shrank to 30 days on 15 September 2026. Nothing here should be treated as fixed forever.

The decision usually comes down to four questions:

1. **Do you want one school for the whole year, or the freedom to switch gyms and travel?** One school points to the ED.
2. **Do you need to keep working remotely for an employer outside Thailand?** That points to the DTV.
3. **Can you show around 500,000 THB in your own account?** If not, the ED may be the only long route open to you.
4. **Months or years?** The ED caps at a year per entry. The DTV runs five.

If you're not sure yet how long you want to train at all, [our Thailand visa guide by training length](/blog/thailand-visa-guide-by-training-length) starts from that question. If the DTV looks like the fit, [check your DTV eligibility first](/dtv-visa/eligibility) before you commit time to building a file.

## FAQ on the Muay Thai education visa and the DTV

### What's the real difference between the ED visa and the DTV?

It comes down to **structure versus flexibility**. The ED ties you to one licensed school with attendance checks and 90-day renewals, for up to a year per entry. The DTV gives you a 5-year, multi-entry visa with no school attachment, room to train wherever you want, and the option to keep working remotely for a foreign employer.

### Can any Muay Thai gym sponsor an education visa?

No. Only gyms licensed as private non-formal schools can, because the embassy needs a certificate from the education authority, such as the Office of the Private Education Commission. Ask to see the licence before you pay.

### How long can you stay in Thailand on a Muay Thai ED visa?

Up to one year per entry: 90 days on the visa, then extensions of up to 90 days each at immigration. Every extension needs proof of the hours you've attended.

### Can you convert a tourist visa or visa exemption into an education visa in Thailand?

Sometimes, at an immigration office, if you still have at least 15 days left on your current stay. With the visa exemption cut to 30 days since 15 September 2026, that window is tight, and local guides report it's rarely possible in Chiang Mai. The DTV has no in-country option at all: you apply from outside Thailand.

### Can you work on a Muay Thai education visa?

No. The ED exists for study only and does not authorize work. The DTV allows remote work for a foreign employer, but not a job or clients in Thailand.

### Do you need the same proof of funds for the education visa as for the DTV?

No. The DTV sets a clear benchmark of around 500,000 THB. ED requirements vary by embassy and school. For the DTV figure and what counts, [check your DTV eligibility](/dtv-visa/eligibility).

## Conclusion

There's no universally "better" visa, only a better fit for how you want to live and train. The Muay Thai education visa suits someone who wants one school, a fixed timetable and a year in one place, and doesn't mind the 90-day rhythm. The DTV suits someone who wants to move between camps, work remotely, and build a file around a real, ongoing training plan.

## What does a DTV visa agent do?

A DTV visa agent mostly fills in the same online form you could fill in yourself, then checks your documents before you submit. That's the core of the service, whatever the marketing around it says. Some agents also translate documents, or walk you through what to bring to the appointment if your embassy requires one.

What an agent doesn't have is privileged access to the embassy, or any "fast lane" through the system. Every application, agent-assisted or not, goes through the same government channel: the [official Thailand e-Visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th), the only system the Thai government runs for this visa. An agent submits your paperwork through the identical portal you'd use yourself, and the embassy reviews it the same way either way.

Once you see it that way, the fee makes more sense. You're not buying a shortcut or an inside track, you're paying someone to do the data entry and the document check for you. That's a real service, just a narrower one than most people assume, on the same **DTV visa application** you could complete on your own.

## What DTV visa agents cost

DTV agents typically charge between **30,000 and 80,000 THB**, on top of the visa itself. Some applicants report current agent rates starting around 30,000 THB, climbing well past that depending on how much handling they expect included.

Compare that to the official embassy fee: a flat **10,000 THB**, non-refundable regardless of outcome, according to [The Thaiger's DTV visa guide](https://thethaiger.com/guides/visa-information/thailand-digital-nomad-visa). An agent's fee alone can run three to eight times that base cost, before you've paid the embassy a single baht. That's not a moral judgment on agents. It's just the number, put next to the number it sits on top of, so you can weigh it before committing.

Nothing about this is hidden. Agencies are generally upfront about their rate once you ask. What most applicants underestimate isn't the price itself, it's what that price does and doesn't include, especially since **applying for the DTV without an agent** is a realistic option for a large share of candidates.

## Can you apply for the DTV yourself?

Yes, for a standard file: clear funds in your own personal bank account, a real soft power enrolment, and a coherent story, the process is straightforward enough to handle alone. This covers the majority of applicants, not just the simplest edge cases.

The core steps are short: gather your documents (passport, bank statements, soft power enrolment letter), complete the application on the [official Thailand e-Visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th), pay the 10,000 THB fee, and submit from outside Thailand, since the DTV can only be applied for from abroad, never converted from inside the country once you've arrived on another visa. For the full walkthrough with document checklists and timing, see [the complete step-by-step application guide](/dtv-visa/how-to-apply).

One thing worth planning around: processing time varies a lot by embassy, from roughly three to seven days at faster posts like Vientiane or Kuala Lumpur, to four to six weeks at busier ones like London, Washington DC, or Tokyo. **Applying for the DTV yourself** doesn't mean applying at the last minute. Build the buffer in, whether or not you use an agent.

## When does hiring an agent make sense?

An agent earns their fee in a handful of real situations, not as a default step everyone should budget for. If your **finances are complicated**, funds split across multiple accounts, money transferred in recently, or income that's hard to document cleanly, a second pair of eyes can catch a mistake before submission rather than after a rejection.

There are a couple of other honest reasons too: a rough immigration history in a third country, or a real **language barrier** with no free help around. And sometimes it's simpler than that. Some people just don't want to spend an evening on forms, and paying for that peace of mind isn't a wrong reason, as long as the price is clear before you commit.

What doesn't hold up is treating an agent as mandatory for a standard situation. If your file is clean and your story is coherent, you're paying for convenience, not necessity, and it's worth knowing the difference before you decide.

## Signals of a fair agent versus one to avoid

A fair DTV agent quotes a clear price upfront, before you send any money, and puts in writing exactly what's included. That's the single easiest signal to check, and the one most worth insisting on before you pay anything, whether you're comparing agents or just **avoiding a bad DTV agent experience** altogether.

Watch for the opposite signals too. No agent, however experienced, can guarantee your DTV visa gets approved: the decision belongs to the embassy alone, and anyone promising a guaranteed outcome is promising something outside their control. A trustworthy agent also has a clear answer, in writing, about what happens to their fee if your application is rejected, separate from the embassy's own non-refundable 10,000 THB charge, which is never returned regardless of who filed it. See [what happens to your fee if you're rejected](/blog/dtv-visa-refund-if-rejected) for the full breakdown of what's refundable and what isn't.

Ask directly what's included before paying: does the quote cover document review only, or also translation and appointment support? Is there a partial refund if the embassy rejects the file? A vague answer to either question is itself a signal.

As Meaw Boonpradub, who guides DTV applicants for free over WhatsApp, puts it: "I'd rather tell someone honestly that their file is straightforward than let them pay 50,000 THB for something I could walk them through in an evening."

## FAQ on DTV visa agents

### How much does a DTV visa agent typically cost?

Reported rates run 30,000 to 80,000 THB, on top of the 10,000 THB embassy fee and any soft power course cost. The exact price depends on how much of the process the agent handles for you.

### Can I apply for the DTV visa without any help?

Yes, if your file is standard: clear personal funds, a real soft power enrolment, and no complicated immigration history. Most applicants in that situation complete the process directly through the official portal.

### Does hiring an agent guarantee my DTV visa gets approved?

No. The embassy makes the approval decision, not the agent. No agent, regardless of price, can guarantee an outcome that belongs to the embassy alone.

### What should I ask a DTV agent before paying them?

Ask exactly what's included in the price, whether translation and appointment support are covered, and what happens to their fee if your application is rejected. A clear, written answer to all three is the mark of a fair agent.

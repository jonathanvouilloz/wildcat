Two new conditions are being added to the **Destination Thailand Visa**, and they land on **31 August 2026**. You now have to apply in a country where you actually belong, and you have to hand over a **criminal record clearance certificate**. Neither is complicated in itself. Together they change the order in which you should plan a long training stay in Thailand.

We prepare DTV enrolment paperwork for trainees at Wildcat every month, so this one matters to us directly. Here is what the announcement says, what it kills, what it does not touch, and what we would tell anyone who was about to book a flight to Chiang Mai with a plan to sort the visa later.

## What the announcement actually says

The [Royal Thai Embassy in Vientiane](https://vientiane.thaiembassy.org/en/page/destination-thailand-visa-dtv) published the notice on 27 August 2026. In its wording, new additional requirements for DTV applications "will take effect worldwide from 31 August 2026", and applicants must:

- Be **a national or permanent resident of the country where the visa application is submitted**.
- Provide **a certificate of criminal record clearance** issued by the authorities of either their country of nationality or the country where the application is submitted.

The embassy then applies both to itself: from that date, people applying in Vientiane must be Lao nationals or permanent residents of the Lao PDR, and must submit a criminal record certificate from Laos or from their own country.

Two things are worth saying plainly. The announcement is an embassy notice, and at the time of writing the other Thai missions had not yet published matching wording on their own sites, so the exact document format each post will accept is not public yet. And the notice describes itself as worldwide, which points to a decision taken above embassy level rather than a local Vientiane rule. Confirm the detail with the embassy you intend to file at before you order anything.

## The part nobody planned for: the neighbouring embassy route is closing

Here is the change that actually bites.

Until now, the DTV had a quiet convenience built into it. You had to apply from outside Thailand, but "outside Thailand" could mean almost anywhere. A German living in Chiang Mai on a tourist stamp could take a cheap flight to Vientiane or a bus to Savannakhet, file there, and come back with a five year visa. Whole visa agency businesses were built on that hop. As recently as early August 2026, a widely used country by country breakdown of DTV practice listed [Singapore as the only post explicitly limiting the visa to its own citizens and residents](https://petchnumnoi.com/blog/2025-dtv-visa-requirements-per-country/), with the rest accepting third country applicants.

From 31 August, that Singapore rule becomes the default everywhere. If you are French, you file in France. If you are Australian, you file in Australia, or in a country where you hold permanent residence. Being physically present in Laos on a tourist entry is no longer enough, because tourist presence is not residence.

This matters more to Muay Thai trainees than it does to remote workers, and the reason is behavioural. People rarely arrive in Chiang Mai having already decided to stay a year. They arrive for three weeks, fall for the training, and then start looking for a way to stay. The Laos hop was what made that late decision possible. After 31 August, deciding to stay long term while you are already here means going home to apply, or accepting a shorter route. If you want the comparison between those routes, [the DTV against tourist visa runs](/blog/dtv-vs-tourist-visa-runs-cost) and [the ED visa against the DTV](/blog/ed-visa-vs-dtv-muay-thai) both cover it.

## The criminal record certificate is a calendar problem, not a moral one

Most people read "criminal record certificate" and assume it is a screening question about their past. For the overwhelming majority of applicants it is not. It is an administrative delay.

A clean record still takes weeks to prove. The document has to be requested from a national authority, it usually involves fingerprints or an identity check, and it arrives on the issuer's schedule rather than yours. Here is what you are looking for, by country, with lead times that were current when we published this and that you should re-check against the issuer before you rely on them.

| Country | Document | Issued by | Typical lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom | ACRO Police Certificate | ACRO Criminal Records Office | Around 2 to 4 weeks standard, with a paid faster option |
| United States | Identity History Summary | FBI | Roughly 2 to 6 weeks, plus getting fingerprints taken |
| France | Bulletin n°3 du casier judiciaire | Ministère de la Justice | Days, requested free online |
| Australia | National Police Check | Australian Federal Police | Around 2 to 3 weeks |
| Canada | Certified Criminal Record Check | RCMP | Around 3 to 4 weeks, fingerprint based |
| Germany | Führungszeugnis | Bundesamt für Justiz | Around 2 to 3 weeks |
| Netherlands | VOG | Justis | Around 2 to 4 weeks |

Three practical points sit on top of that table.

**Language.** The Vientiane embassy already requires that documents in a language other than Thai or English be translated and notarised. A British ACRO certificate arrives in English and needs nothing. A French bulletin n°3 or a German Führungszeugnis will need a translation, which adds its own week.

**Legalisation.** Some posts may want the certificate legalised as well as translated. Thailand deposited its instrument of accession to the Hague Apostille Convention on 30 June 2026, but [the Convention only enters into force for Thailand on 28 February 2027](https://silklegal.com/thailands-accession-to-the-apostille-convention-what-businesses-and-individuals-should-know/). Until then the older consular legalisation chain is what applies, and it is slower than an apostille. Ask your embassy whether they want the plain certificate or a legalised one before you pay for anything.

**Validity window.** Many countries expect a police certificate issued within three to six months of the application. Order yours eight months ahead and you may have to pay for it twice.

## This is not an isolated move

Read on its own, the announcement looks like a sudden clampdown. In the context of 2026, it looks like the next step in a sequence.

The DTV has been tightening steadily since launch. Bank statements now need to show the 500,000 THB seasoned for at least three months rather than parked the week before. General Thai language schools were removed from the qualifying soft power list in 2025, pushing those applicants toward the ED visa. Every application goes through the [official e-Visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th), with no paper channel. And in May 2026 the Cabinet voted to cut the visa exempt stamp from 60 days back to 30 for most nationalities, alongside a cap of two land border entries per calendar year, a change [still waiting on Royal Gazette publication](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/visa-exempt-status-2026-returning-to-30-days) as of late August 2026.

The direction of travel is consistent. Thailand still wants long stay foreigners, and keeps building visa categories to attract them. What it has stopped tolerating is the gap between what a visa says you are doing and what you are actually doing, and the fact that it often has no idea who has walked in. A residence requirement and a background check both fit that reading.

## What it changes if you want to train Muay Thai here

Nothing in the announcement touches the DTV itself. Muay Thai remains a qualifying soft power activity. The 500,000 THB in a personal account, the 10,000 THB fee, the five year validity, the 180 days per entry and the single 180 day extension are all unchanged. So is the practical reality that a training programme of at least six months reads far better than a one month course. If you need those basics, [the DTV eligibility guide](/dtv-visa/eligibility) has them.

What changes is the order of operations.

1. **Decide before you fly, not after you arrive.** The old plan of coming on a tourist stamp and converting later is now expensive, because converting means going home.
2. **Order the police certificate first.** It is the longest lead item in the whole file. Start it before you chase anything else.
3. **Get your gym enrolment paperwork in parallel.** For us that means an enrolment letter, a payment receipt and a programme description covering at least six months. We prepare that for trainees, and it costs nothing beyond the training itself.
4. **File in your own country, through the e-Visa portal.** [The full application walkthrough](/dtv-visa/how-to-apply) covers the rest of the documents.
5. **Then book the flight.** With processing running anywhere from a week to a month on top of the certificate, working backwards from a fixed arrival date is how people end up applying in a panic.

If you already hold a DTV, the announcement does not reach you. It governs new applications at embassies. In country extensions are handled by Thai Immigration, which is a separate process under separate rules.

## What is still unclear

We would rather flag the gaps than fill them with guesses. On a visa question, a confident wrong answer costs you money.

- **What counts as permanent residence.** The notice says national or permanent resident. It does not say whether a long term work permit, a spouse visa or a student residence permit in a third country will be accepted. Read strictly, they would not be.
- **What the certificate has to look like.** Format, age limit, translation and legalisation requirements are not specified in the announcement.
- **How applications already in progress are handled.** Files submitted before 31 August are presumably assessed under the old rules, but the notice does not say so.
- **Whether every post applies it on the same date.** The notice says worldwide from 31 August. Embassy practice has historically varied, so verify with yours.

## Our take

We think this is a sensible change with bad timing, and we would say that even though it makes our own trainees' lives harder in the short term.

Sensible, because the neighbouring embassy hop was always a soft spot. It let people treat a five year visa as an impulse purchase, and it fed an agency industry that charged three or four times the visa fee to walk someone through a form. Anything that pushes applicants toward preparing a real file, in their own country, on their own timeline, raises the quality of the applications and reduces the number of people who arrive with a plan built on a loophole. We have written before about [gyms that overcharge for DTV help](/blog/muay-thai-gym-dtv-overcharging), and this rule removes some of the ground those operations stand on.

Bad timing, because four days of notice for a worldwide document requirement is not enough for anyone mid-plan. Someone who booked a Vientiane appointment for next week, or who is three weeks into a two month stay and has just decided to turn it into a proper training block, has no good option left. That person did nothing wrong.

Our position for trainees is simple. Treat the DTV as a decision you make at home, months ahead, with a police certificate ordered on day one. And if that timeline does not work for the trip you have already booked, come and train on the stay you have, see whether the camp suits you, then apply properly from home for the long one. A five year visa is worth waiting a few weeks for, and we are not going to pretend otherwise to get someone through the door faster.

## FAQ on the new DTV requirements

### When exactly do the new DTV requirements start?

The Royal Thai Embassy in Vientiane's announcement of 27 August 2026 states that the additional requirements take effect worldwide from 31 August 2026.

### Can I still apply for the DTV in Laos?

Only if you are a Lao national or a permanent resident of the Lao PDR. Being in Laos on a tourist entry no longer qualifies you to apply there once the rule takes effect.

### Does this affect a DTV I already hold?

No. The announcement covers new applications submitted to Thai embassies and consulates. An existing DTV keeps its validity, its 180 days per entry and its in country extension, which Thai Immigration handles separately.

### Which criminal record certificate does Thailand want?

One issued by the authorities of your country of nationality, or of the country where you file the application. The announcement does not specify a format, an age limit or whether translation and legalisation are required, so confirm those points with the embassy you will apply at.

### Is Muay Thai still a qualifying activity for the DTV?

Yes. Muay Thai training remains on the soft power list, and nothing in this announcement changes that. [The DTV for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) covers how the soft power route works in practice.

### I am already in Thailand and want to switch to a DTV. What can I do?

You cannot apply from inside Thailand, and from 31 August you cannot use a neighbouring embassy unless you are a resident there. The realistic options are to apply from your home country, or to look at a different route such as the ED visa or a tourist extension while you plan.

## Conclusion

The DTV is not harder to qualify for than it was last week. It is harder to obtain casually. The visa still runs five years, Muay Thai still counts, and the money threshold has not moved. What has moved is where you can file and how long the paperwork takes to assemble, and both of those push the whole process back to your home country and several weeks earlier in your planning.

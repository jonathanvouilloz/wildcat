Every November the same questions reach us on WhatsApp: when is it, can I release a lantern, and is the big paid event worth it? Here is a plain answer to **the Yi Peng festival in Chiang Mai** for 2026, written by a family-run Muay Thai camp in Hang Dong, not by a ticket seller. We earn nothing from any of the events below.

One thing first, because it surprises people: our own district, Hang Dong, has been a no-release zone for lanterns. We explain why in a moment. Everything here comes from pages we opened on 14 November 2026, and where only one source says something, we say so. Plans in Chiang Mai change late, so confirm on the day.

## Yi Peng 2026 at a glance: dates, what is confirmed and what is not

**Confirmed.** The Tourism Authority of Thailand (TAT) states that this year the full moon falls on 24 November. Loy Krathong is held on the full moon of the twelfth lunar month, so the main night is **Tuesday 24 November 2026**. Chiang Mai's airport also frames the lantern nights as 24 and 25 November.

**Reported by one source.** A Thai travel report from 3 November 2026, crediting TAT Chiang Mai, describes the city programme like this:

- A main festival from 23 to 25 November.
- On 23 November, a procession that forms behind Tha Phae Gate and walks to Wat Chedi Luang in the early evening.
- On 25 November, 15 giant krathong floats travel from Tha Phae Gate to the municipality office.

The same article says the main site moves away from Tha Phae Gate this year. We could not find that anywhere else, so do not plan around it.

**Not found.** We could not find the municipality's own 2026 programme, and neither the 2026 provincial lantern announcement. If you read a different date elsewhere, that is normal: some sites list 23 to 25 November, others 24 to 26. Look for the city's own notices closer to the day. For the wider picture of the season, see our guide on the [best time to train Muay Thai in Thailand](/blog/best-time-train-muay-thai-thailand).

## Khom loi, khom fai, krathong: what is what, and why Lanna people do it

Three words get mixed up, so here is the short version.

- **Yi Peng** is the festival of the full moon of the second month of the Lanna calendar. The word *yi* means two and *peng* means full moon day. It is the same night as the twelfth month in the central Thai calendar, which is why it overlaps with Loy Krathong.
- **Khom loi** is the sky lantern: paper or fabric on a light frame, lifted by hot air.
- **Krathong** is the small decorated float you set on water. In Chiang Mai the two celebrations run together as one multi-day event.

**Khom fai** is where sources disagree. Wikipedia describes khom fai as decorative lanterns hung at homes, gardens and temples, while the Thailand Foundation uses khom fai as another word for the sky lantern. If a page uses the term, check which one it means.

The meaning matters more than the word. The festival began in the Lanna kingdom and is mostly Buddhist: people make merit, and releasing a lantern is a way to let go of past bad luck and send a wish into the new year. The krathong honours Phra Mae Khongkha, the water goddess, and many people use it to apologise for polluting the water. Nobody needs a lantern to take part in any of that.

## Can you release a lantern? The rules, and why Hang Dong has been off limits

Short answer: only where it is officially allowed, and in recent years that has not included where we live.

The reason is the airport. Chiang Mai's international airport is close to the city, and thousands of lanterns drift through the take-off and landing paths. The airport's general manager has said that releases are "absolutely prohibited" in the red zone along those paths, and that over 200,000 sky and smoke lanterns are expected in the airport safety zone. He asked people to follow the provincial announcement.

Here is what we know about the zones:

- **2024.** The province announced a total ban across six districts, including all of Hang Dong and all of Mueang Chiang Mai. Releases were allowed only outside the safety zone, on set evenings, with the district chief's permission requested in advance.
- **2025.** The Thaiger reported the same six districts as red zones, again with Hang Dong in full, plus an extra zone around the airport. Time Out reported that the CAD event was one of the few approved, with lanterns that must break down within a year.
- **2026.** We could not find the provincial announcement. So we cannot tell you which districts are on the list. Hang Dong has been on it every year we could check, so we plan as if it is again.

The penalties are serious. Press reports of the province's warnings say an unpermitted release can mean up to five years in prison, a fine of up to 200,000 THB, or both, and more if it starts a fire. We have not checked the statute itself, so treat those as reported figures.

There is also a knock-on effect for travellers. As of 30 October, for 21 to 28 November, the airport counted 108 cancelled flights, 80 retimed and 105 extra flights added. In 2025 the figures were 65 cancelled, 96 retimed and 44 extra. Airlines have been asked to avoid 10:00 to 12:00 on 24 November and 19:00 to 01:00 on 24 and 25 November. That is a request to airlines, not a legal closure, but it cuts the airport's working day from about 20 hours to about 12. If you fly that week, check your booking and arrive early: the airport advises two hours before a domestic flight and three before an international one.

## Ticketed release or free celebration? What you actually get

**The ticketed release.** CAD Khomloy is the best known. TAT confirms it for 24 and 25 November at the CAD Cultural Center Lanna in Mae On, outside the city. We could not find an organiser website. The only pricing we found is on a reseller page, which lists tickets from 4,900 THB (Standard) up to 15,900 THB (Elite). It says each ticket includes two lanterns, one krathong, food and drinks, monk chanting and transport from town, with the launch at about 20:15. When we looked, every tier and both dates showed as out of stock and sales were final.

That is a lot of money for about half an hour of lanterns. It can be a lovely evening, and it is a legal way to see a mass release, but you are paying for a staged spectacle. If you want it, check the organiser's terms yourself and do not trust an unofficial seller blindly.

**The free side.** The city's own celebrations are open to anyone: the procession, the giant krathong floats, temples hung with lanterns and the riverside. We could not verify a day-by-day free list for 2026, so use the official programme once it is out. A good evening can cost a bowl of noodles and a short walk.

**Where we land.** We would rather spend the evening with the city than with a ticket. You see families, monks, children and old neighbours doing something that is theirs, and you pay nothing. We do not think a paid release is wrong. We think the free version is closer to what the festival is.

## Krathong done right, and practical tips

**Krathong.** Thailand's Pollution Control Department asked people in November 2025 to stop using bread krathong, use banana leaves or similar, share one krathong per family, and float only in designated areas. Foam is the real problem. After Loy Krathong 2022 the Irrigation Office reported about 15 tonnes of krathong pulled from the Ping river in Chiang Mai, 3% of it foam. In 2023 it was 33 tonnes, 98% biodegradable. Natural materials and one per group are a good rule.

**The river.** In 2025 the Ping rose before the festival and flooded some municipal krathong piers, and officials urged visitors to stay careful on the banks. Keep children back from the edge and check the water level that day.

**Getting around.** Expect busy roads, closed streets near the old city and slow rides. Book a ride away from the busiest streets, or walk the last stretch. We found no verified 2026 closure plan or price data for Grab, so we are not guessing.

**Fire.** Hot wax, candles and paper do not mix with crowds. Hold your candle low, keep it away from other people's clothes and hair, and put it out before you leave.

**Temples.** Dress modestly, keep your voice down and do not step over candles or offerings. If you are not sure, watch what local families do.

## FAQ: Yi Peng and Loy Krathong in Chiang Mai

### When is Yi Peng 2026?

The full moon falls on 24 November 2026, so the main night is that Tuesday. A Thai report on the city programme lists the festival as 23 to 25 November. The ticketed CAD release is listed for 24 and 25 November.

### Can I release a sky lantern in Chiang Mai?

Only where the province allows it. All of Hang Dong and Mueang were no-release zones in 2024 and 2025, and we could not find a 2026 list. Check the provincial announcement first.

### Is the ticketed CAD event worth it?

It is a staged release outside town, listed from 4,900 THB, and it showed as sold out when we looked. The free city celebrations are a good alternative.

### What is the difference between Yi Peng and Loy Krathong?

Yi Peng sends lanterns up and is tied to the Lanna calendar. Loy Krathong sets floats on water. In Chiang Mai they run together.

### Are krathong bad for the river?

Foam ones are. Banana leaves and flowers are better, and the Pollution Control Department asks people to stop using bread.

If you are planning to move to Thailand with a partner or kids, the **DTV Dependent** route is either very simple or an immediate brick wall, depending on one document: a legal marriage certificate. Legally married spouses and unmarried children under 20 can join a primary DTV holder with no cap on family size. Long-term **unmarried partners**, on the other hand, are excluded at almost every Royal Thai Embassy and need a different plan before paying a non-refundable fee.

I'm Meaw, founder of Wildcat Muay Thai in Chiang Mai. Because Wildcat is a family-run, open-air camp rather than a hardcore fight gym, we get messages every week from couples and parents trying to figure out the Thailand DTV spouse and dependent route. This guide walks through who actually qualifies, how unmarried couples work around the restriction cleanly, how embassies handle the **500,000 THB** financial rule for households, and whether you should apply together or one after the other. If you have not read the baseline visa rules yet, start with [our full DTV visa guide](/dtv-visa).

## Married, unmarried, or with kids: who qualifies as a DTV dependent?

Under Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa framework published via the [official Thailand e-Visa portal](https://www.thaievisa.go.th) and detailed by [Siam Legal](https://www.siam-legal.com/thailand-visa/dtv-visa-thailand.php), the primary applicant must be at least 20 years old. Once the primary applicant qualifies under Workcation or Thai Soft Power, dependent eligibility breaks down cleanly into three groups:

| Family member | Eligible as DTV Dependent? | Separate 10,000 THB fee? | Relationship proof required | Financial proof reality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legally married spouse | Yes | Yes | Official marriage certificate (often translated to English + apostilled/legalised) | Varies by embassy: either main holder's 500,000 THB + sponsorship letter, or separate/higher combined funds |
| Unmarried children under 20 | Yes (no official cap on number of kids) | Yes, per child | Official birth certificate naming the parent (translated + legalised if requested) | Covered by the main parent's financial proof + sponsorship letter |
| Unmarried partner / boyfriend / girlfriend | No (almost universally refused) | N/A | N/A | Must qualify independently as a second primary DTV applicant |
| Civil union / PACS / de facto partner | Embassy-dependent (frequently refused) | Yes, if accepted | Official civil union certificate | High risk of refusal; confirm in writing with your embassy before paying |

Notice that last row carefully. Some European applicants hold a registered civil partnership (such as a French PACS or a UK/Australian de facto registration) and assume it counts the same as marriage. In practice, many Thai embassies strictly require a formal marriage certificate and reject civil unions. Never guess on this point: email the specific embassy covering your country of residence before paying the 10,000 THB fee, or treat your file as two separate primary applications. You can also run your household setup through our quick tool to [check your DTV eligibility](/dtv-visa/eligibility).

## The unmarried partner trap and how couples actually solve it

Every month, an unmarried couple writes to us after realizing they cannot share a single DTV. One partner usually has a clean salaried remote job for Workcation, while the other is freelancing, taking a break, or working locally in a role that does not produce a clean remote-work letter. They assume the second partner can be added as a dependent, only to discover the embassy requires a marriage certificate.

Do not try to force a joint dependent application with a lease agreement or joint bank statement. Embassies routinely reject those files and keep the 10,000 THB application fee.

The clean, reliable workaround used by unmarried couples moving to Chiang Mai is straightforward: **apply as two independent primary DTV holders**. That usually looks like one of two setups:

- **Split categories**: Partner A applies via Workcation (using their salaried employment contract), and Partner B applies via Thai Soft Power (for example, a genuine 6-month Muay Thai training programme). Both receive their own independent 5-year multiple-entry DTVs, and neither visa depends on the other.
- **Both via Soft Power**: If both partners are freelancers with informal paperwork or taking a career break together, both enrol in a recognised Soft Power activity and submit clean, parallel primary applications.

If you are deciding which primary category fits each partner best, read [our Workcation vs Soft Power DTV comparison](/blog/dtv-visa-types-workcation-vs-soft-power) before booking anything.

## The 500,000 THB question: per person or per household?

This is the single biggest grey area for legally married couples and families, and honest guides admit it upfront: **Thai embassies do not apply one uniform rule for dependent financial proof**.

Here is what happens in practice across different missions:

1. **The sponsorship model**: Many embassies accept one seasoned personal bank account holding at least 500,000 THB in the primary applicant's name, paired with a signed sponsorship letter stating the primary holder will financially support their spouse and children in Thailand.
2. **The per-adult or scaled-balance model**: Stricter embassies ask to see 500,000 THB per adult applicant (1,000,000 THB combined for a married couple) or a larger household balance when multiple children are included, even when the spouse applies as a dependent.

Because this varies by embassy and can change between officers, verify your local embassy's checklist before you upload. If you have the savings available, showing a stronger combined balance across 3 months of clean personal bank statements removes the easiest reason for a follow-up request. Keep in mind that the rules on *what* counts as valid liquid funds (personal savings vs crypto, investments, or company accounts) remain identical for families. We cover those exact asset rules in [our DTV proof of funds guide](/blog/dtv-visa-proof-of-funds).

## Documents, legalisation, and timing: together or one after the other?

Preparing a dependent file takes a bit more lead time than a solo DTV because civil status documents often need translation or legalisation. For each dependent, expect to prepare:

- **Valid passport** with at least 6 months of validity remaining and blank pages.
- **Passport-style photo** meeting e-Visa portal specifications.
- **Proof of relationship**: Official marriage certificate for a spouse, or official birth certificate for each child under 20. If the document is not in English or Thai, get a certified English translation, and check whether your embassy requires an apostille or ministry legalisation stamp.
- **Copy of the main holder's DTV**: Either the approved e-Visa PDF (when filing sequentially) or the main applicant's reference/proof of eligibility (when filing together).
- **Financial proof and sponsorship letter**: Main holder's 3-month bank statements (500,000 THB+ or higher per embassy rules) plus a signed letter confirming financial responsibility.
- **Criminal record certificate and residence proof**: Since 31 August 2026, adult applicants must apply through the embassy covering their country of nationality or legal residence and provide a criminal record certificate. Check whether your embassy requires a criminal record check for an adult dependent spouse as well (children are typically exempt, though always confirm locally). See [the new DTV rules from 31 August 2026](/blog/dtv-visa-new-requirements-2026) for full details.
- **Separate application fee**: 10,000 THB equivalent per dependent, paid individually on the e-Visa portal.

### Should you apply together or sequentially?

According to [ExpatDen's DTV guide](https://www.expatden.com/thailand/destination-thailand-visa/), dependents can technically submit their applications at the same time as the primary applicant at many embassies. However, in real-world practice, **filing sequentially is often safer** unless you are under a tight flight deadline.

Why? Because if the primary applicant's Workcation or Soft Power file gets rejected or delayed for missing paperwork, every dependent application linked to it fails automatically, and none of the 10,000 THB fees are refunded. If you apply for the primary DTV first, wait 5 to 15 working days for the approval PDF, and then attach that approved visa directly to your spouse and children's dependent applications, you protect the rest of the family's fees and give the embassy officer an instant green light on the relationship link.

## FAQ on DTV spouse and dependent visas

### Can an unmarried couple get a DTV visa together in Thailand?

Not under the Dependent category. Almost all Thai embassies require a legal marriage certificate for a spouse dependent. Unmarried partners who want to move to Thailand together on a DTV should each apply as an independent primary applicant, using Workcation, Muay Thai Soft Power, or a mix of both.

### Do civil partnerships like a French PACS qualify for DTV Dependent?

It depends entirely on the specific Royal Thai Embassy, and many missions refuse civil unions in favour of formal marriage certificates. Never submit a dependent application based on a civil partnership without getting written confirmation from your embassy first.

### What happens if my child turns 20 while holding a DTV Dependent visa?

The Dependent category covers unmarried children under 20 at the time of application. Once a child turns 20, they will no longer qualify for a new dependent visa and will need to transition to their own independent visa (such as their own primary DTV, since 20 is the minimum age for a main DTV applicant, or a Non-ED student visa) when their current visa or stay requires renewal.

### Does every family member have to pay the 10,000 THB embassy fee?

Yes. Each spouse and each child under 20 submits an individual application through the Thailand e-Visa portal and pays their own non-refundable 10,000 THB (local currency equivalent) application fee.

## Conclusion

The Thailand DTV spouse and dependent route works smoothly when you respect two clear boundaries: legal marriage is required for a partner to apply as a dependent, and every family member needs their own individual application and fee. If you are legally married or moving with kids under 20, consider getting the primary DTV approved first before submitting dependent files so you never risk multiple fees at once. If you are an unmarried couple, plan two independent primary files from day one.

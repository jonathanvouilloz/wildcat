Yes, some Muay Thai gyms are overcharging for DTV papers. Not because the paperwork is complicated. Because most DTV candidates have no idea what a "soft power training package" is supposed to cost, so almost any number sounds plausible.

Here's the fact that started this conversation: the same documents, an enrolment letter, sometimes an attendance log, are priced anywhere from **7,500 to 25,000 THB** depending on the gym. Same paperwork. More than triple the price, at the top end.

I'm Meaw. I own and run Wildcat Muay Thai in Chiang Mai, and I write these enrolment letters myself, for every trainee who asks. I know exactly what they cost to produce, because I produce them. This article is my honest answer to a question I now get several times a month on WhatsApp: **are Muay Thai gyms overcharging for DTV papers**, and if so, how do you tell?

## What's actually in a "DTV soft power" package?

Muay Thai is one of the [officially recognised soft power activities](https://www.muaythaivisathailand.com/soft-power-and-the-destination-thailand-visa-for-muay-thai-students/) that qualify you for the DTV. But the "package" a gym sells you for it is, in almost every case, one or two pieces of paper. Not a legal service, not embassy access, not a guarantee. If you haven't already, it helps to understand [the DTV visa for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) as a whole before you compare gym prices against each other.

Here's what a gym genuinely provides:

1. **An enrolment letter** confirming you're signed up for a long-term training program, signed by the owner or head coach.
2. **Sometimes an attendance record**, showing you actually train, not just that you paid.
3. **Nothing else.** No "visa processing," no embassy liaison, no fast-track review, no approval guarantee.

That's the entire deliverable. The embassy still makes the decision. The gym's role is to document that you're a real training candidate, not a name on a fake enrolment list. When a gym prices its "DTV package" like it's a legal filing service, that mismatch between what's delivered and what's charged is worth noticing.

## Why prices range from 7,500 to 25,000 THB for the same documents

Prices vary this widely because of how the paperwork gets bundled, not because the paperwork itself changes. Three patterns explain most of the gap.

**First, mandatory long packages.** Some camps only issue DTV documents alongside a required training block, often six months paid upfront, even if you only want one month of actual training before you leave to apply. You end up paying for months you didn't ask for, folded into the "DTV price."

**Second, a disconnected admin markup.** A handful of gyms add a flat "processing fee" for the letter itself, unrelated to the few minutes it takes to draft and sign a one-page document.

**Third, treating DTV candidates as a separate product.** Some camps run one price list for regular trainees and a higher, DTV-specific rate for anyone who mentions "visa." That's the clearest version of overcharging: identical training, different sticker price, based on what the paperwork is for.

For context, regular **Muay Thai gym prices in Chiang Mai** for monthly unlimited training typically sit in the 4,000 to 8,000 THB range. If a gym's "DTV package" costs two or three times its own regular monthly rate for the same or fewer sessions, that gap is the number worth asking about.

## "A month is all that's needed": is that actually true?

The honest answer is: it depends on your full file, not just the training length. This claim shows up often, usually from someone who wants to book the shortest, cheapest package possible.

As our own internal visa review puts it: "Thai immigration practice through 2025-2026 has favored programs of six months or more, though this is an observed pattern, not a published legal rule." Shorter enrolments get more scrutiny. That pattern is real, but there's no statute saying "one month = automatic rejection." The embassy weighs your entire application, not just the duration on one letter.

So a gym isn't lying if it offers a one-month enrolment letter. It's misleading if it markets "one month, guaranteed solid file," because that overstates what any single document can do. The real variable isn't how many weeks you paid for. It's whether your full file, proof of funds, travel history, stated plan, hangs together. A one-month letter next to a thin file looks like a shortcut. The same letter next to a coherent six-month plan reads very differently.

No gym controls the embassy's decision. Be wary of anyone who implies otherwise. ## Signals of a transparent gym vs an opportunistic one

The clearest signal is whether the price is public before you've handed over your contact details. A transparent gym publishes its rate card, DTV documents included, on its own website. An opportunistic one asks you to "message us for a quote" and prices you differently depending on how motivated you sound.

Beyond that, look for these signals:

- **The DTV document price isn't higher than the gym's normal training rate.** No hidden "visa surcharge" bolted onto the regular fee.
- **The gym answers clearly** when you ask exactly what the letter covers, and what it doesn't.
- **No promise of visa approval.** The final decision sits with the [official Thai e-visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th), not the gym, and any camp implying it can "guarantee" your DTV is overselling its role.
- **A clear policy if your visa is refused.** Ask upfront whether training fees are refundable if the application doesn't go through; a straight answer here tells you a lot.

Before you pay any gym for DTV papers, ask these four questions:

1. What exactly does this price cover, the letter, the training, or both?
2. Is this the same rate as regular training, or a separate "DTV" surcharge?
3. If my visa is refused, what happens to what I've paid?
4. Can I see an example of the enrolment letter, with personal details removed?

## FAQ on Muay Thai gyms and DTV papers

### How much should DTV Muay Thai training documents cost?

A DTV enrolment letter is one to two pages and should reflect the real cost of preparing it, not a separate "visa product" fee. At a transparent gym, it's included in your regular training price. If a gym quotes a DTV-specific price well above its own normal rate for the same documents, that's worth questioning.

### Is it normal for a gym to charge extra just for DTV paperwork?

No. An enrolment letter and, sometimes, an attendance record takes a gym owner minutes to prepare. A fair gym folds this into its standard training price. A separate "DTV surcharge" on top of regular fees is a signal to ask more questions.

### Do I have to train for 6 months to get valid DTV documents?

Not legally, no. Six months or more is the pattern Thai immigration has favored in practice through 2025-2026, but it's not a published legal threshold. What matters more is whether your full application, funds, plan, and documentation, is coherent.

### What happens if my DTV visa is rejected after I've paid a gym?

That depends entirely on the gym's own refund policy, since no camp controls the embassy's decision. A transparent gym will tell you clearly, before you pay, whether training fees are refundable if your application is refused. Avoiding the question is itself a signal.

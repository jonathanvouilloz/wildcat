Every year around February, the same question lands in our WhatsApp: *"Is it true you can't breathe in Chiang Mai?"* Here is the honest answer, from a family that trains outdoors year-round.

## What burning season actually is

From roughly **late February to mid-April**, agricultural burning across northern Thailand, Laos and Myanmar pushes PM2.5 levels up. Some days are fine, some days are genuinely bad. Anyone telling you it's "no big deal" — or that the city is unlivable — is selling you something.

## The honest month-by-month calendar

| Month | Air quality | Training outdoors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November – January | Excellent | Best months of the year |
| February | Variable | Mostly fine, check daily |
| March | Often poor | We adapt — see below |
| April (early) | Poor to variable | Songkran usually clears it |
| May – October | Good (rainy season) | Green, lush, quiet |

## How we adapt training at the camp

On bad-air days we shift sessions earlier, lower the cardio volume and move technical work under the covered ring. The pool helps too. Nobody pretends it's ideal — but nobody stops training either.

## So when should you book?

If you have full flexibility, **November to January** is the sweet spot. If your dates fall in March, message us first — we'll tell you honestly what the week looks like before you commit.

Before the first kick, there is the stance. Everything in muay thai — power, balance, defence — starts from how you stand. Here is how we teach it at the camp, in the order your first coach will.

## The stance

Feet shoulder-width, lead foot pointing at your opponent, back heel slightly raised. Weight 50/50, knees soft. If you can't bounce lightly in place, you're too planted.

## The guard

Hands high, elbows in, chin down. The classic beginner mistake: dropping the rear hand while jabbing. Every coach on earth fixes this one daily — you won't be the exception, and that's fine.

## Footwork before fists

Step and slide — never cross your feet. Forward: lead foot first. Backward: rear foot first. Ten minutes of footwork drills feel boring until the first time someone moves at you and your body answers on its own.

## The jab

Rotate the fist at the end, snap it back faster than it went out, and keep breathing. Power comes from the hips later; for now, the jab is a measuring tool, not a weapon.

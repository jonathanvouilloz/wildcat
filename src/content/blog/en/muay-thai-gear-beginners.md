Every week, someone arrives at the camp with a duffel bag full of gear they bought based on YouTube reviews. Shin guards still in the packaging. A brand-new pair of 16 oz gloves. Sometimes a full headgear set. And the first thing I tell them is: you didn't need any of that yet.

This guide is what I wish someone had shared before they booked their flight. A real packing list for your first Muay Thai camp in Thailand, built on what hundreds of beginners actually needed when they walked through our gates in Chiang Mai.

---

## The thing no gear guide will tell you

Gloves and hand wraps are **free to borrow at the camp**.

At Wildcat, we lend them to every new student. Clean, sized, ready to use. This is true at most reputable camps in Thailand, and it changes the entire packing calculation.

Why does this matter? Because the most common beginner question is "what are the best beginner muay thai gloves to buy?" The honest answer: don't buy any before you've trained in a few pairs. After one week at the camp, you'll know exactly what weight feels right for your hands, what style of wrist strap you prefer, and whether you want a Thai-made pair (available everywhere in Chiang Mai, for a fraction of Western prices) or a specific brand you've researched. Buy then. Not before.

The gear industry has an obvious interest in sending you to the shop before you travel. We have the opposite interest. We want you to arrive light, feel comfortable, and spend your energy on training, not on managing a heavy bag.

## What you genuinely need to pack

### Workout clothes (2-3 sets minimum)

Muay Thai training is two sessions a day in humid tropical heat. You will sweat more than you expect. Pack enough to rotate without doing laundry every night.

Shorts first. Muay Thai-specific shorts are not required for day one. Any athletic shorts that allow a full range of hip movement work fine. If you want the real thing, **muay thai shorts in Thailand** cost 150-350 THB at any market near the camp. Buying locally is half the fun: you'll find the same quality as Western-priced gear for a third of the cost.

For your top layer, a fitted rash guard or a close-cut athletic shirt. Loose cotton T-shirts get caught during clinch work and soak through in ten minutes. A moisture-wicking or synthetic fabric makes the morning session a lot more bearable. For women, a sports bra with good support under a rash guard is the standard at the camp.

Socks are for travel and daily life, not for training. You train barefoot on the mat.

### Flip-flops

You will wear them everywhere: gym to shower, shower to breakfast, breakfast to the street. If yours are falling apart, buy a new pair before you go, or grab them at the first 7-Eleven you pass in Chiang Mai.

### A small towel

Gyms in Thailand often provide towels, but a fast-drying travel towel of your own is useful for early morning sessions before the laundry rotation catches up.

### Water bottle

Bring a refillable one. The camp provides water, but having your own bottle means you drink more. Thai heat catches people off guard, and how much you drink has a direct effect on how you feel in the second session of the day.

### Basic first aid

Nothing exotic. A small roll of athletic tape (for minor blisters or to reinforce wrapping on sore joints), antiseptic cream, and blister plasters. Pharmacies in Chiang Mai are well stocked, so you can resupply easily, but having a small kit in your bag saves you a trip on day two when your feet are unhappy.

## What you do NOT need to bring

### Gloves

Already said it, but worth repeating: the camp lends gloves. If you decide mid-stay that you want your own pair, buy them in Thailand. Fairtex, Yokkao, Twins, Top King. All made here. The same models that cost 60-80 USD online cost 1,200-1,800 THB (roughly 30-50 USD) at a local shop. You'll get a better deal and you'll have had time to feel what size and style suit you.

### Hand wraps

Also available to borrow. If you already own a pair and they fit easily in your bag, bring them. But if you're packing from scratch, skip it.

### Shin guards

Not used in beginner pad work. They come into play once you're doing sparring, which for most first-timers on a 2-4 week trip may not happen at all. If your stay extends and you start sparring, buy a pair locally.

### Headgear

Same logic as shin guards. Not needed for pad rounds. If sparring becomes part of your training, your coach will tell you and you can sort it on the spot.

### Mouth guard

This one is worth owning. It's small, cheap, and hygienic. You don't want to borrow one. If sparring is on your plan at all, pick one up before you leave. If you're coming purely for pad work and conditioning, it can wait.

### A full training bag

The big branded gear bags you see on equipment websites are useful once you live at a camp long-term. For a first trip of 1-4 weeks, a regular daypack or the side pocket of your main luggage is enough. Your gloves are borrowed, your shin guards are borrowed, your shorts weigh nothing.

## Buying gear in Chiang Mai

Chiang Mai has excellent gear options and you'll be better positioned to choose after a few days of training.

Hand-sewn shorts, basic wraps, and entry-level gloves are available at Warorot Market and most sporting goods shops in the Hang Dong area. For under 1,000 THB you can kit yourself out in authentic Thai training gear.

For branded Thai gear, Fairtex has a retail presence in Chiang Mai. Yokkao, Twins, and Top King have resellers throughout the city. Expect to pay 1,200-2,500 THB for a solid pair of beginner gloves, depending on the brand and weight. For a beginner, 10 oz for bag work and pad work, 12-14 oz once sparring is involved. The coaches will steer you right once you're here.

**Muay Thai shorts in Thailand** are genuinely worth buying locally. The quality is high, the designs are better than most imports, and the prices make no sense to replicate elsewhere. Budget 200-400 THB for a solid pair, more for premium embroidered styles.

## The full packing checklist

**Bring from home:**
- 2-3 sets of athletic clothes (shorts + rash guards or fitted tops)
- Flip-flops
- Travel towel (fast-drying)
- Refillable water bottle
- Mouth guard (if sparring is on your plan)
- Small first aid kit (tape, antiseptic, blister plasters)

**Available to borrow at camp:**
- Gloves
- Hand wraps

**Buy in Chiang Mai after a few days of training:**
- Muay Thai shorts (150-400 THB)
- Your own gloves, if you decide to (1,200-2,500 THB)
- Shin guards, when sparring begins

**Leave at home entirely:**
- Shin guards (for a first visit)
- Headgear (for pad work only trips)
- Heavy gear bag

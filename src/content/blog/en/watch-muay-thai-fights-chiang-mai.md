If you are wondering **where to watch Muay Thai fights in Chiang Mai**, the short answer is that you have four regular choices, each with a different feel, and you can usually find a show on any night except Sunday.

We are a family-run camp in Hang Dong, so this guide is written by people who train Muay Thai every day, not by a ticket seller. That also means we can be straight about one thing: we do not run any of these stadiums, and we earn nothing from your ticket. Every day, time and price below comes from a page we actually opened on 29 September 2026, and each one is named so you can check it yourself. Schedules in Chiang Mai move around, so treat this as a map and confirm on the day.

## Chiang Mai's Muay Thai stadiums at a glance

Chiang Mai has one of the easiest [Muay Thai in Chiang Mai](/) scenes to dip into: fights start at 21:00, tickets are cheap next to the beach towns, and most venues are close to the old city. Here is what each venue lists.

| Stadium | Days listed | Show time | Seats listed (THB) | Feel | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chiang Mai Boxing Stadium | Monday to Saturday | 21:00 to about 23:00 or 23:30 | 600 standard, 1,000 ringside, 1,500 VIP (one guide) | Larger, air-conditioned, north of the old city | Seeing local fighters |
| Thaphae Boxing Stadium | Monday to Saturday | 21:00 to 23:45 | 600, 1,000, 1,500 VIP | Small shed by Tha Phae Gate | First-timers, photos |
| Kalare Boxing Stadium | Monday to Saturday | 21:00 to 23:45 | 600, 1,000 | Inside the Night Bazaar | Dinner plus a fight |
| Loi Kroh Boxing Stadium | Varies by month | 21:00 | 600, 1,000 (one guide) | A ring in a bar alley | A casual, lively night |

Where these numbers come from, and where they disagree:

- **Chiang Mai Boxing Stadium** advertises "Mon-Sat 21:00", a show until 23:30 and tickets "from 600 baht" on its own site (Sorying Arena, opened 29 September 2026). The 1,000 and 1,500 THB seat prices, and the earlier 23:00 finish, come from [Muay Thai Chiang Mai](https://muaythaichiangmai.com/chiangmai-boxing-stadium), a ticket-affiliate guide whose page is dated 13 July 2026.
- **Thaphae** lists Monday to Saturday, 9:00 to 11:45 PM, with VIP at 1,500 THB (one drink included), ringside at 1,000 and a stadium seat at 600, on [its own site](https://www.thaphaeboxingstadium.com) (opened 29 September 2026). That settles the question of whether it runs every day but Sunday: it does not list a Sunday show.
- **Kalare** lists ringside at 1,000 THB and a stadium seat at 600 THB, Monday to Saturday from 9:00 to 11:45 PM, on [its own site](https://www.kalareboxingstadium.com) (opened 29 September 2026).
- **Loi Kroh** is the one that moves. The guide above, updated 7 September 2026, lists Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and its Loi Kroh page (updated 29 April 2026) adds that extra nights appear in some months. A ticketing page we saw earlier listed Monday to Saturday. The [official Loi Kroh calendar](https://www.loikrohboxingth.com/watch-a-fight) we opened on 29 September showed nights on Wednesday 30 September, Friday 2 October, and Monday 5, Wednesday 7 and Friday 9 October, each from 21:00 to midnight. We could not find an official Loi Kroh price on that page, so the 600 and 1,000 THB figures are the affiliate guide's, dated 29 April 2026.

There are also two seasonal venues. The same guide lists Anusarn Market on Thursdays and a Pavilion venue on Sundays, both marked "in some months, currently closed" on 7 September 2026. Do not plan a night around either without checking the day itself. We looked for the Kawila stadium mentioned in some older guides and found nothing current, so we left it out.

## Chiang Mai Boxing Stadium and Thaphae Boxing Stadium

These two are the ones the affiliate guide points to first when you ask about tonight, because both list fights Monday to Saturday.

**Chiang Mai Boxing Stadium** is the big one. Its own site says it sits in the Chang Phueak area, near Chang Phueak Market, at 177 Changphuak Road, and it describes weekly matches with boxers from Bangkok, other regions and abroad. The affiliate guide adds that it is the largest stadium in the city, the only one that is air-conditioned, and that many fights feature young local fighters on their way up. It also says the venue is one of the few that locals come to, mostly on weekends. That guide's own words are that you should not expect life-or-death fights, but you can see authentic Muay Thai. If your main question is "will I see real local fighters", this is the one that guide recommends.

**Thaphae Boxing Stadium** is by Tha Phae Gate on the east side of the old city, at Mun Mueang Road according to its own site. The guide describes a large shed with open seating, no tiers, and a small enough room that the view is good from most seats. It also mentions live traditional music, and some extra entertainment between bouts, such as sword fighting and blindfolded boxing. The same guide says the audience is mostly foreigners. That matters for how you choose, and we come back to it below.

For a first fight with friends, Thaphae is the easy pick: it is close to the old city's guesthouses, the seats are close to the ring, and a standard ticket is enough. If you care about photographing the ring lights and the crowd, the low, open room helps.

## Loi Kroh, Kalare and the newer venues around Night Bazaar and Anusarn

**Kalare Boxing Stadium** sits inside the Kalare Night Bazaar, between the old city and the Ping River. The affiliate guide calls it a small stadium with nothing fancy, about a five-minute walk from the old city, convenient for shopping and dinner before the show, with mostly tourists in the seats. If you want an easy evening that combines a night market and a fight, this is the natural match. The same guide lists about 6 to 7 fights per night.

**Loi Kroh Boxing Stadium** is a different kind of place. The guide describes it as less a stadium than a boxing ring between bars in a covered alley, with the audience on plastic chairs. It adds that drinks and food are pricier than at the other venues, and warns that the alley itself is full of nightlife that some visitors find a bit uncomfortable. Its own site says it has been running for over 25 years, from Loi Kroh Road Lane 3, and that it also offers training and a gear shop. For a relaxed, bar-side evening it can be great fun. For a family night, look at the other options first.

The newer **Anusarn Market** venue is in the southern part of the Night Bazaar area, and, per the same guide, it runs a Thursday show that is sometimes closed. It is worth a look only if you find it listed for the night you are there.

## Tourist show or real fights? How to tell, and which ticket to buy

Almost every fight night in town is built for visitors. That is not a scam, it is just how the market works. The useful question is not "real or fake" but "what kind of night do I want".

A few signs you can read yourself, before you buy:

- **The crowd.** A guide we read says Chiang Mai Boxing Stadium is the venue that regularly draws local spectators, mostly on weekends, while the other stadiums lean tourist-oriented. If you would like a more local crowd, that is the tip to follow.
- **The card.** The same guide says other venues can feature international fighters against clearly weaker locals, and even novelty bouts, and that quality varies from night to night. If the promotion advertises novelty matches, you know what you are getting.
- **The order.** Both guides describe cards that start with young fighters and women's bouts and finish with the senior men's fights, usually 6 to 8 bouts in total. Expect the main event late, and note the first bout is sometimes delayed.
- **The weekend.** The affiliate guide says Friday and Saturday are the busiest nights and usually carry the best fights.

None of this makes a tourist-friendly card a bad night out. It just means it is a show you enjoy as culture, rather than a serious fight card. If you already follow Muay Thai and want technical fights, you will get more out of the Bangkok scene, which we compare in [Chiang Mai vs Bangkok for Muay Thai](/blog/chiang-mai-vs-bangkok-muay-thai).

**Which seat should you buy?** Listed prices are 600 THB for a standard seat and 1,000 THB for ringside. Some venues list a 1,500 THB VIP seat, which at Thaphae is an air-conditioned room with a drink included, according to its own site. The affiliate guide describes standard seats at Thaphae as floor-level chairs, often by a table, and ringside as cushioned chairs in the rows nearest the ring, better for atmosphere but "not everyone might find them worth it".

Our plain advice on the best stadium for Muay Thai in Chiang Mai to buy for:

1. **First-timer or on a budget:** a 600 THB standard seat. The guide says the small venues offer a good view from anywhere.
2. **You want photos or to feel the kicks:** ringside at 1,000 THB.
3. **It is hot and you want to sit down in comfort:** look at the VIP option at a venue that lists it, or pick the air-conditioned one.

Tickets for a Chiang Mai boxing stadium are widely sold online, and every guide we read advises booking ahead. Book through the venue's own page when you can, and compare it with an affiliate price, since we saw both listed.

If you would like to understand what you are watching, read [the wai kru and ram muay you see before a fight](/blog/wai-kru-ram-muay-explained), and [the mongkhon](/blog/muay-thai-headband-mongkhon), the headband the fighters wear into the ring. Both make a night at the stadium more fun.

## Getting there from Hang Dong, and getting back

Wildcat is in Nong Kwai, Hang Dong, outside the old city, so a fight night means a trip in and a trip back. We are not going to guess a travel time, because it changes with the time of day and traffic. Open your maps app with your venue's address from the sections above, or ask us for the current price and duration before you go.

Your realistic options are:

- **Grab or a taxi.** The simplest choice if you plan to have a drink, and the one we suggest for the ride home. Check the price in the app before you commit.

Two practical points. Shows are listed until about 23:00 to 23:45, and Loi Kroh's listings run to midnight, so plan your return before the last bout starts, not after. And our last group class of the day ends at 20:00, which leaves you time to shower and eat before a 21:00 start. We cannot promise a departure time, since it depends on the day's class and traffic.

## FAQ on Muay Thai fights in Chiang Mai

### Are there Muay Thai fights in Chiang Mai tonight?

Very likely, unless it is a Sunday. Several venues list fights Monday to Saturday at 21:00. Check the venue's calendar for the date, because some venues, like Loi Kroh, only run on certain nights and seasonal ones close for months.

### How much do tickets cost?

Listed prices are mostly 600 THB for standard, 1,000 THB for ringside and about 1,500 THB for VIP where offered. These were the figures on the venues' own pages and one affiliate guide on 29 September 2026. Confirm the price when you book.

### What time do the fights start and how long do they last?

Listings say 21:00, with the show running about two to three hours. The first bout can start late.

### Is there a dress code?

None of the venue pages we opened states one. If you are unsure, ask the venue when you book.

### Are the fights fake?

One guide describes most venues as tourist-oriented, with uneven matchups, and Chiang Mai Boxing Stadium as the one closest to a local fight night. Every venue is different and quality varies by night, so read the card, and go on a Friday or Saturday if you can.

### Should I book ahead?

Yes, the guides we read recommend booking online in advance, particularly for ringside and weekend nights.

## Conclusion

Chiang Mai has a fight most nights, and the main choices come down to the kind of evening you want: local fighters at Chiang Mai Boxing Stadium, a first-timer's shed at Thaphae, a market-and-fight combo at Kalare, or a bar-alley night at Loi Kroh. Prices are low next to the beach towns, but days and times move, so confirm on the venue's own page before you go. Plan your ride home first, and the night takes care of itself.

## Camp profiles: beginner and beginner-friendly

### Wildcat Muay Thai (Hang Dong)

I'll keep this factual. You can judge the rest.

**Wildcat** is an open-air camp in Hang Dong, a quiet area south of the city. The ring is outdoors under a pavilion, surrounded by a garden. Classes run every morning and evening, seven days a week. Levels are separated: beginners train in a structured track, so you are not competing with experienced students for pad time in your first week.

Good fit if you are:

- A complete beginner who wants a real progression structure, not "train with everyone and figure it out yourself."
- Planning one month or more and want affordable training, a daily routine, and a place that feels like a home rather than a hotel gym.
- Applying for a DTV visa, or already on one. I handle the training documentation personally.
- Training with children, or as a family. Parents and kids can be on the same session.
- Someone who watched intimidating fight camp videos on YouTube and was relieved to find this is not that.

Prices: drop-in 400 THB, monthly training from 8,000 THB. [Stay and train packages](/stay-train) cover longer stays with help finding accommodation near camp.

Not a good fit if you want a beach, a nightlife scene, or a deep international fight team. Hang Dong is quiet by design. For competitive fighters preparing for professional bouts, the fight-team depth of Phuket is not here, and that is honest.

### Dang Muay Thai (Old City)

**Dang** is the most-reviewed camp not just in Chiang Mai but in Thailand, with over 3,000 five-star Google reviews. The facilities are modern: roughly 1,600 square metres, two rings, 29+ bags, 16 full-time trainers who are ex-champions. 90-minute structured classes.

Location is its main selling point: Old City, walkable to restaurants, markets, and Chiang Mai nightlife. Accommodation is available on-site with AC, included weekly private session, and daily cleaning.

Good for beginners who want a polished, structured environment in the city centre. Some guests find the scale leans more professional than personal, which is the usual trade-off at a high-volume operation. Visa ED support is available (annual package, premium price).

Prices: drop-in 350-450 THB, monthly 5,000 THB (1x/day) to 9,000 THB (2x/day).

## Camp profiles: all levels and resort

### Hongthong Muay Thai (Mae Hia)

**Hongthong** sits about 15-20 minutes from the city and airport, near the Mae Hia / Hang Dong corridor. The installation is substantial: a pro ring, sauna, ice bath, and a cluster of three partner hotels within walking distance (~60 rooms total). There is a cafe and smoothie bar.

The training pedigree is genuine: Kru Joe (Hongthongnoi) and Kru Gen (Hongthonglek) are twins with 700+ combined professional fights and Lumpinee titles. The camp specialises in clinch and muay khao, which Joe organises as Hongthong Fight Nights. Kru Bee is the female coach, consistently mentioned by female guests as an asset.

Avis consistently use the word "family" and "home." The ratio of clinch instruction to general striking is higher than most camps, which some guests come specifically for.

Prices: drop-in ~300 THB, monthly 6,000 THB (1x/day) to 9,000 THB (2x/day).

### The Camp (Mae Hia / Hang Dong)

**The Camp** was historically linked to Fairtex. The current model is semi-private: a low student-to-trainer ratio, with more individual corrections than group-class-at-scale. Options include Lite (2 sessions/day) or Full (4 sessions/day), detox and weight-loss programmes, and visa support.

Monthly training ~12,000 THB. Accommodation is resort-grade and premium-priced. No alcohol on site. Good for people who want a fully structured routine in an all-inclusive environment; less good for those who want flexibility or a local camp feel.

## Camp profiles: traditional and fighter-focused

### Santai Muay Thai (San Kamphaeng)

**Santai** is the reference for traditional Muay Thai in the north. It sits in the countryside east of the city, surrounded by rice fields. That isolation is part of the design.

Trainers come from the Pinsinchai lineage (Bangkok), with Lumpinee and Rajadamnern titles. The camp can take a beginner to a first five-round fight, but the honest caveat from multiple guest accounts is that beginners can feel overlooked if the camp is focused on fighters at the time. The website says "all levels" and that is technically true; the experience at beginner level varies.

Prices: drop-in 500-600 THB, weekly 3,000 THB, monthly ~10,000 THB.

Not the first choice for: a true first-timer or someone looking for a fitness/holiday experience. The best fit is an intermediate who wants technical development and is comfortable being a long way from the city.

### Lanna Muay Thai / Boon Lanna (Saraphi)

**Lanna Muay Thai** (also known as Kiatbusaba / Boon Lanna) is a separate and distinct camp from Tiger Muay Thai Chiang Mai. Do not confuse the two.

It is one of the most historically significant camps in northern Thailand, founded in the 1960s and among the first in the north to open to Western students. Today it is run by Kru Boon, who grew up there. The training is traditional Muay Thai — mae mai technique, fight culture, and an older coaching lineage. There is no MMA or BJJ.

The camp has a long history with visa ED and can support long-stay applications. Accommodation is in construction. Guest accounts consistently mention that experienced coaches remember individual students across visits, sometimes years apart.

Prices: drop-in 400 THB, weekly from 2,000 THB (1x/day), monthly ~8,800 THB (2x/day).

Good for: long-stay students who want the culture of Muay Thai alongside the training, traditional technique, and an established visa process. Not "fancy" by resort standards. The draw is the lineage and the atmosphere, not the facilities.

### Sit Thailand (near Chiang Mai airport)

**Sit Thailand** carries the Pinsinchai lineage through Kru Thailand Pinsinchai, a Golden Era champion. Sessions are serious and technically focused. The camp sits near a resort with a pool.

Drop-in 600 THB, monthly ~10,000 THB. Aimed at intermediate to advanced students and competitive fighters. Less suited to beginners looking for a gentle introduction.

## Camp profiles: resort and premium

### Tiger Muay Thai Chiang Mai (San Sai / Mae Rim)

**Tiger Muay Thai Chiang Mai** is a branch of the Tiger brand (the major Phuket operation) located north of the city in San Sai / Mae Rim, roughly 18-24 km from the centre. It is a forest retreat with full resort infrastructure: ring, weight room, pool (extra), MMA classes, yoga, and deluxe accommodation.

This is not the same camp as Lanna Muay Thai. Tiger Chiang Mai is a separate operation, newer and resort-oriented.

For training: monthly from 16,300 THB (training only), with deluxe stay-and-train packages around 31,000 THB/month per person. Day pass 800 THB, private session 800 THB.

Good for: students who want all disciplines under one roof (Muay Thai, MMA, yoga), a comfortable natural setting, and a recognisable brand. The most expensive option in this guide. Some guests describe it as a "spa-holiday" experience as much as a training camp. The trade-off for the premium is that the environment is less intimate than smaller family camps.

### Gym Bangarang (Mae Rim)

**Gym Bangarang** is positioned as the beginner-plus-wellness option, also in Mae Rim. It runs HYROX / functional fitness alongside Muay Thai, includes three meals per day, laundry, and airport transfer in its packages. DTV-certified.

All-inclusive packages rather than à la carte pricing. Good for beginners who want a structured, "no-rush" environment with wellness components. No unsupervised sparring early on. Less suited to those who want city access or a fighter atmosphere.

## Camp profiles: budget and urban

### Muay Thai Fever (Suthep)

**Muay Thai Fever** sits at the Almag Sports Complex in Suthep, between the city and Doi Suthep mountain, with a running track and synthetic pitch nearby. Monthly unlimited training at 5,500 THB is among the best rates in Chiang Mai. Trainers are certified (A License Boxing Board, WMC, IFMA). Ajarn Burklerk Pinsinchai, a Golden Era double Lumpinee champion, trains there part-time.

Prices: 5,500 THB/month unlimited, stay and train ~16,000 THB/month. Strong DTV support. Good for all levels, nomads, and those who want a family feel without the premium resort price.

### The Bear Fight Club (Pa Daet)

**The Bear** relocated from the Old City to Pa Daet (near the airport) in late 2025. It is an old-school, small-group gym with long sessions (up to 3 hours in the afternoon). Drop-in 300 THB, monthly from 4,000 THB. No accommodation. One of the best price-per-hour ratios in the north. Good for anyone on a tight budget who wants volume.

### Chiang Mai JR Muay Thai (Nimman)

**Chiang Mai JR** is urban and climate-controlled, near Maya Mall in Nimman. The indoor space is an asset during burning season (March-April) when outdoor training across the city becomes difficult. Good for digital nomads who structure training around work hours. No accommodation on site; the Nimman area has plentiful options but at higher rental prices than the suburbs.

## Women training Muay Thai in Chiang Mai

This gets two lines in most articles, and two lines are not enough.

**Wildcat is woman-run.** I am the owner, I set the culture, and I coach. Mixed training is standard: women train alongside men at all levels without separation or implied hierarchy. On most mornings, roughly half the people hitting pads are women. Different countries, different fitness levels, different reasons for being here.

At a woman-run camp, the culture is set from the inside. Coaches do not condescend. No one stares at you during your first session. You are not "accommodated." You belong.

**Hongthong** has Kru Bee as a dedicated female coach, which multiple women cite specifically as why they chose the camp. **Dang** has a consistently female-welcoming reputation in guest accounts.

Chiang Mai has a noticeably stronger environment for women than the large fight camps in Phuket. The city is historically one of the strongest locations in Thailand for competitive women's Muay Thai.

## Families and children

This is the smallest niche and the least well served in Chiang Mai.

**Wildcat** has a dedicated kids and family programme where parents and children can train on the same session. That is unusual. Most camps have no structured children's offer.

**Hongthong** is family-friendly and comfortable for families staying nearby, though the kids' programme is less formal.

If you are travelling with children and Muay Thai is part of the plan, Wildcat is the clearest option in the city.

## The DTV visa: the structural edge Chiang Mai has over Phuket

This is the most important factor if you plan more than 60 days, and it is barely covered in most camp comparisons.

The **Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)** is a 5-year, multi-entry visa that allows stays of up to 180 days per entry. Muay Thai training is a qualifying activity. To apply, you need documentation from your camp: an enrolment letter, training schedule, and attendance confirmation that Thai immigration officers accept.

**Not every camp handles this.** Some will give you a letter that looks right but does not meet the standard.

How the visa timeline maps to your camp decision:

- Under 30 days: tourist exemption, any camp works
- 30 to 90 days: tourist visa (TR), choose on preference
- 90 days to 5 years: the DTV is your cleanest path, and your camp choice matters

Camps with confirmed DTV support (based on available information): Wildcat, Muay Thai Fever, Gym Bangarang. Lanna Muay Thai has a long history with visa ED specifically. Dang offers visa ED (annual package, higher price point). Always confirm current DTV support directly before booking.

For the full process, read the [DTV visa for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) guide and the step-by-step [how to apply for the DTV](/dtv-visa/how-to-apply) walkthrough.

## What to actually look for when comparing camps

**How much pad time do you get per session?** At a camp with 80 students and 10 trainers, the math is not in your favour. Ask directly: how many students per trainer in a typical session?

**Does the camp have a beginner track?** Some camps absorb beginners into the general group. That is fine for a confident intermediate, rough on day one. Ask what your first week looks like specifically.

**What is included in the monthly fee?** At Wildcat, gloves and wraps are available to borrow and there are no hidden add-on fees for standard training. Not all camps work this way.

**Can they provide DTV training documentation?** "Yes we can help" and "yes we have submitted real DTV dossiers and know what the file needs" are different answers.

**Where will you live?** The camp should be able to point you toward specific accommodation options. Wildcat works with nearby condo landlords and can guide you on realistic rent ranges. See [our stay and train packages](/stay-train#packages).

## FAQ on Muay Thai camps in Chiang Mai

### What is the best Muay Thai camp in Chiang Mai for beginners?

Wildcat, Dang, Hongthong, Muay Thai Fever, and Gym Bangarang all have good beginner tracks. Wildcat has the structured beginner programme and the most family-oriented environment. Dang has the best facilities in the city centre. The [beginner Muay Thai classes](/classes/beginners) page covers what the first weeks at Wildcat look like.

### Is Lanna Muay Thai the same as Tiger Muay Thai Chiang Mai?

No. Lanna Muay Thai (Kiatbusaba / Boon Lanna) is a historically significant camp in Saraphi, founded in the 1960s, focused on traditional Muay Thai under Kru Boon. Tiger Muay Thai Chiang Mai is a separate resort-style camp in San Sai / Mae Rim, a branch of the Tiger Phuket brand, offering MMA, yoga, and a full resort experience. They are distinct operations in different locations.

### How much does it cost to train at a Muay Thai camp in Chiang Mai?

Monthly unlimited training runs 5,500 to 16,300 THB depending on the camp. Drop-in sessions run 300 to 600 THB. Add accommodation (5,000 to 9,000 THB/month), food (6,000 to 10,000 THB/month), and a scooter (3,000 THB/month) for a realistic total of 25,000 to 38,000 THB per month.

### Is Chiang Mai or Phuket better for Muay Thai training?

For long-term stays, beginners, DTV applicants, and budget-conscious training: Chiang Mai. For short trips (1 to 2 weeks), elite fighter training, beach lifestyle, and large international fight teams: Phuket. See the [full comparison article](/blog/chiang-mai-vs-phuket-muay-thai).

### Are there female-friendly Muay Thai camps in Chiang Mai?

Yes. Wildcat is woman-run. Hongthong has a dedicated female coach (Kru Bee). Dang has a consistently positive reputation in female guest accounts. Chiang Mai generally offers a more welcoming environment for women than the large fight camps in Phuket.

### Can I train Muay Thai in Chiang Mai on a DTV visa?

Yes, and Chiang Mai is the better city for it. Wildcat, Muay Thai Fever, and Gym Bangarang have confirmed DTV support. Lanna has a long history with visa ED. The DTV allows 180-day stays on a 5-year multi-entry visa, with Muay Thai as a qualifying activity. Read the full process on the [DTV visa for Muay Thai](/dtv-visa/muay-thai) page.

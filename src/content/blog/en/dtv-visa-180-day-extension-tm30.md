Most people assume the **DTV 180-day extension** is a quick 1,900 THB rubber stamp at immigration, only to get turned away at the counter because their landlord never filed a TM30 or they did not bring fresh 500,000 THB bank statements.

## How the DTV 180-day rule actually works: once per entry, not once in 5 years

Let's clear up the most common misunderstanding first. The DTV is a **5-year multiple-entry visa** granting up to 180 days per entry. Each 180-day entry can be extended **once per stay** at a Thai Immigration office for an additional 180 days (giving you up to 360 continuous days in Thailand) for 1,900 THB.

That "extendable once" wording trips people up constantly. It does **not** mean you only get one extension across the entire five years of your visa. As confirmed by the Royal Thai Embassy in London and [ExpatDen's DTV guide](https://www.expatden.com/thailand/destination-thailand-visa/), once you reach the end of an extended 360-day stay (or after your initial 180 days if you choose not to extend), you simply exit Thailand and re-enter on the same valid DTV before it expires. Immigration stamps you in for a brand-new 180-day period, which is again eligible for one 180-day extension.

Because the DTV is multiple-entry by design, you **never need a re-entry permit** when leaving Thailand for a trip or a stay reset, according to [Siam Legal's DTV overview](https://www.siam-legal.com/thailand-visa/dtv-visa-thailand.php). Just remember that since 1 May 2025, every foreign traveller entering Thailand must complete the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) online before landing.

## Documents for the 1,900 THB DTV extension at Chiang Mai Immigration

If you decide to stay put in Thailand past day 180 rather than flying out, you apply in person at your local immigration office (for Chiang Mai residents, Chiang Mai Immigration, where counters and annex locations occasionally shift, so check the current extension desk a week before going). Apply around 15 to 30 days before your 180-day stamp expires; you do not lose those remaining days because the new 180 days start from the end of your current stamp.

According to the Immigration Bureau document checklist reported by ExpatDen, bring a complete physical folder with:

- **Form TM.7** (Application for Extension of Temporary Stay), filled out cleanly with a recent 4x6 cm passport photo attached.
- **1,900 THB in cash** (exact change helps).
- **Passport and signed copies**: Photo/data page, your DTV e-Visa printout, and your latest arrival stamp page.
- **Fresh proof of 500,000 THB**: Bank statements (from your foreign bank account, a Thai bank account, or combined) showing at least 500,000 THB held for **at least 30 days** before the extension application date. Alternatively, a formal company guarantee or support letter covering at least 500,000 THB. You do not need a Thai bank account for this step; see [our guide to opening a Thai bank account on a DTV](/blog/dtv-visa-thai-bank-account) and [DTV proof of funds rules](/blog/dtv-visa-proof-of-funds) for what accounts work cleanly.
- **Proof of ongoing category activity**: Immigration does not just check your first entry; they want to see you still qualify under the category that granted your DTV. That means updated remote-work/freelance evidence for Workcation, an updated Muay Thai or Soft Power training certificate/enrolment confirmation for Soft Power, or relationship proof for Dependents.
- **Proof of residence**: Signed condo lease, hotel receipt, or house registration documents.
- **TM.30 receipt and previous TM.47 slip**: Copies of your address registration receipt and your 90-day report receipt.

That re-verification of your 500,000 THB and ongoing category proof is the detail that surprises people. At Wildcat, we issue updated attendance and ongoing training documentation for active students extending in Chiang Mai, so you are never scrambling at the last minute.

## TM30 and the 90-day report (TM.47): the two traps that stall extensions

Even with a perfect bank balance, two routine Thai immigration forms cause almost every headache we see at Chiang Mai Immigration.

### TM30: address registration within 24 hours

The **TM30** (Notification from House-Master, Owner, or Possessor of the Residence) is legally your landlord's, condo manager's, or hotel's responsibility within 24 hours of your arrival. In reality, you are the one who suffers if they forget, because Chiang Mai Immigration routinely refuses to process a 90-day report or a 180-day extension without a current TM30 receipt stapled or printed in your folder.

Whenever you check into a new apartment or return to Chiang Mai from an international trip, ask your landlord or condo juristic office for a screenshot or printout of the filed TM30 receipt within your first 48 hours. If you stay with us through our Stay & Train programme, we handle your TM30 registration directly so your folder stays clean.

### The 90-day report (TM.47)

If you stay in Thailand for more than 90 consecutive days without leaving the country, you must file a **90-day report** (form TM.47) confirming your current residential address. This applies to DTV holders just like long-term Non-Immigrant visa holders.

- **Filing window**: You can report up to 15 days before or 7 days after your 90-day due date in person at immigration (online reporting is also available after your first in-person report, though the portal can be patchy).
- **Late fine**: Missing the window typically triggers a **2,000 THB** administrative fine when you go in voluntarily (and up to 5,000 THB if flagged during an enforcement check).
- **Automatic reset**: Every time you leave Thailand, your 90-day counter stops and resets to Day 1 upon your next entry. If you take a trip abroad every two or three months, you will never even reach day 90.

## 1,900 THB extension vs a short international flight: which is smarter?

Once you already hold a DTV, you are not doing risky tourist border runs (if you are still weighing whether to get a DTV at all versus doing tourist entries, read [our DTV visa versus tourist visa runs on cost breakdown](/blog/dtv-vs-tourist-visa-runs-cost)). Here, you already have a valid 5-year multiple-entry visa and are simply choosing between extending in Chiang Mai for 1,900 THB or taking a quick round-trip flight out of Chiang Mai International Airport (CNX) to a nearby hub like Vietnam, Malaysia, or Laos.

| Factor | 1,900 THB in-country extension | Quick international round-trip flight on DTV |
|---|---|---|
| Direct cost | 1,900 THB fee + photocopies/photos (~2,100 THB total) | ~3,500 to 8,000 THB round-trip flight + 1-2 nights hotel if staying overnight |
| Paperwork required | Heavy: TM.7, 500k THB (30-day history), updated category proof, lease, TM.30, TM.47 | Minimal: valid passport, DTV e-Visa printout, TDAC before returning |
| Time spent | Half a day to a full day in queues + document prep | A long weekend away (or same-day turnaround) |
| Effect on 90-day report | Keeps ticking (you must keep filing TM.47 at day 180 and 270) | Resets your 90-day report clock back to Day 1 |
| Stay granted | +180 days (up to 360 continuous days total) | Fresh 180-day entry (which can later be extended by another 180 days) |
| Best fit | Tight budget, settled routine, paperwork already tidy and ready | Busy remote workers or trainees who want a mini-holiday and zero immigration paperwork |

In practice, many of our long-stay students in Chiang Mai mix both: they take a cheap regional flight around day 170 when they feel like a beach break or a trip to Hanoi, and only sit through the 1,900 THB Chiang Mai Immigration queue when they specifically want to stay grounded through a training block or fight camp. Factoring one or two short regional flights a year into your [monthly budget for long-stay training in Chiang Mai](/dtv-visa/long-stay-training) keeps your schedule stress-free.

## Two separate 180-day clocks: visa stay vs Thai tax residency

This is strictly factual information, not tax advice (we are a Muay Thai camp, not tax accountants), but every DTV holder needs to keep two completely different 180-day numbers separate in their head:

1. **Your immigration clock (180 days per entry)**: Controls how long you can legally stay in Thailand on a single entry stamp before extending or exiting. Leaving Thailand for a weekend resets this clock.
2. **Your Thai tax residency clock (180+ days per calendar year)**: Under Thai Revenue Department rules, spending 180 days or more in Thailand within a single calendar year (1 January to 31 December) makes you a Thai tax resident for that year, regardless of how many times you flew in and out on your DTV.

A quick flight to Kuala Lumpur resets your visa stay, **not** your calendar-year day count for tax purposes. If you plan to spend more than 180 days in Thailand in a calendar year, speak with a qualified cross-border tax advisor about how Thailand's foreign-sourced remitted income rules apply to your situation.

## FAQ on DTV extensions, TM30, and 90-day reporting

### Can I extend my DTV for 180 days only once in 5 years, or once per entry?

Once per entry. Every time you enter Thailand on your 5-year DTV, you receive a 180-day stay that can be extended once at Thai Immigration for another 180 days (up to 360 continuous days). After exiting and re-entering Thailand, you get a fresh 180-day stay and can extend again if needed.

### Do I need to show 500,000 THB again to extend my DTV in Thailand?

Yes. Thai Immigration lists proof of at least 500,000 THB (in Thai or foreign bank accounts, held for at least 30 days before applying, or an equivalent guarantee letter) as a required document for the 1,900 THB in-country extension.

### Do I need a re-entry permit to leave Thailand on a DTV?

No. The DTV is a multiple-entry visa. You can leave and re-enter Thailand freely throughout its 5-year validity without buying a re-entry permit. Just complete the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) before flying back in.

### Does my landlord need to file a new TM30 after I return from a trip abroad?

Yes, in Chiang Mai you should always ask your landlord or condo office to re-confirm or re-file your TM30 whenever you re-enter Thailand from abroad or change addresses. Having a fresh TM30 printout prevents immediate rejection at the immigration counter later.

## Conclusion

Staying compliant on a DTV in Chiang Mai comes down to three simple habits: grab your TM30 receipt every time you move or return from abroad, track day 90 if you stay continuously, and decide before day 160 whether you want to assemble the full 1,900 THB immigration folder or take a short flight out to reset both clocks cleanly.

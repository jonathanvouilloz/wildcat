Almost every training trip starts with the same question: which month should I go? We get it on WhatsApp all year, usually from someone with a flight search open in another tab. The honest answer is that **the best time to train Muay Thai in Thailand** depends on where you train, how much heat you can handle, and how long you plan to stay.

This guide comes from Wildcat, an open-air camp with a garden and shade in Hang Dong, near Chiang Mai. So the north gets the most detail. We also cover the south, because the seasons there are not the same. Everything about weather is a typical pattern, not a promise: some years run hotter, wetter or smokier than the average.

## The short answer: best months to train Muay Thai in Thailand

If you only read one thing, read this: for Chiang Mai and the north, **the best month to train Muay Thai in Thailand** falls between November and February. For Phuket and the Andaman coast, it is roughly December to March. Koh Samui, on the Gulf side, has its own rhythm and tends to be wettest at the end of the year.

Here is the pattern month by month. The weather columns follow the general climate of each region. The crowd and price column shows tendencies, not fixed rules.

| Period | North (Chiang Mai) | South (Phuket, Koh Samui) | Crowds and prices |
|---|---|---|---|
| November | Dry season starts, mornings cooler, Loy Krathong and Yi Peng | Phuket turning drier; Samui still rainy | Busy and rising |
| December | Dry, coolest mornings of the year | Phuket good; Samui still wet at times | Peak season |
| January | Dry, cool mornings, warm afternoons | Good on both coasts | High |
| February | Dry, warming up, haze can start | Good on both coasts | High, easing late |
| March | Hot, burning season risk | Hot, mostly dry | Quieter |
| April | Hottest month, burning season may linger | Hot, first storms possible | Quiet, Songkran mid-month |
| May to June | Rainy season starts, afternoon showers | Phuket wetter, Samui often fine | Low |
| July to September | Green, rainy, some heavy days | Phuket wet, Samui usually drier | Lowest |
| October | Rains ease late in the month | Phuket still wet, Samui getting wetter | Low, then picking up |

Nothing here is a hard line. A dry week in September or a rainy one in January is normal. If you want the full north versus south picture, our comparison of [Chiang Mai vs Phuket for Muay Thai](/blog/chiang-mai-vs-phuket-muay-thai) goes deeper on training culture, not only weather.

## November to February: the cool season, best for first-timers

The cool season is when northern Thailand is dry and the air is at its friendliest. Mornings can feel properly fresh, afternoons are warm without being brutal, and open-air training feels good instead of punishing. For a first trip, this is the safest bet: you spend your energy on learning, not on surviving the heat.

The trade-off is demand. Everyone else has read the same weather chart, so flights, rooms and camp spots fill up. If your dates fall in this window, plan earlier than you think you need to.

If you have never trained before, this is also the easiest time to start. Our page on [a first class at Wildcat](/classes/beginners) walks through what happens from the moment you arrive. Group classes run at 09:00, 15:30, 17:00 and 18:30, so you can pick the part of the day that suits you and your body.

### Chiang Mai in November: Yi Peng and Loy Krathong 2026

**Chiang Mai in November** comes with a festival. Loy Krathong falls on Tuesday 24 November 2026, according to calendar tables (the full moon of the twelfth lunar month). In Chiang Mai it overlaps with Yi Peng, the lantern festival, which is why the city gets so busy that week.

Here is what we can and cannot tell you:

- The ticketed sky-lantern release, CAD Khomloy, is listed for 24 and 25 November 2026 by its organiser, with tickets listed between 4,900 and 15,900 THB when we checked on 29 September 2026. That is the organiser's information, so check it before you buy anything.
- The city's own programme for Yi Peng and Loy Krathong 2026 is not confirmed. Some sites say 23 to 25 November, others 24 to 26. We would rather say that than guess.
- Releasing your own lantern is not a free-for-all. For Yi Peng 2025, the province banned releases in all of Mueang Chiang Mai (the city, old town included) and all of Hang Dong, where our camp is, plus parts of four other districts, with penalties of up to five years in prison or a 200,000 THB fine. The 2026 rules had not been published when we wrote this, so check them locally before you light anything.

Our practical advice is simple. Train in the morning, then go out in the evening. See [our daily class schedule](/classes#schedule) and pick a session that leaves your evening free. If you want ideas for the rest of your week, [our Chiang Mai guide](/chiang-mai-guide) has the basics. We are not promising any specific event, because dates can shift.

### Chiang Mai in December: peak season, book early

**Chiang Mai in December** is the sweet spot for the weather and the hardest month for availability. Days are dry, mornings can be genuinely cool, and the city is at its most comfortable for walking around after training.

It is also peak season. Rooms, scooters and camp places go quickly, and prices for flights and accommodation tend to be at their highest around Christmas and New Year. We will not give you a fill rate, because it changes every year. Lock in your accommodation and your training dates as early as you can, ideally before you book flights.

## March and April: heat and the burning season

These are the two months we are most careful about. Chiang Mai gets hot, and it is also the core of the burning season, when agricultural fires across the region and the valley's geography can push air quality down for days at a time.

We would rather show you the numbers than soften them. In 2026 the city reached an AQI of 231 on 2 April, and on 4 April the Ministry of Interior declared Chiang Mai, Lamphun and Phayao emergency disaster zones because of the air quality. No official measures for 2027 had been announced when we wrote this, so we cannot tell you how next year will go. Our camp is open-air, and we do not pretend it is spared: on bad days we adapt sessions.

If your dates land here, read [the burning season in Chiang Mai](/blog/burning-season-chiang-mai) first. It explains what changes at the camp, who should avoid this period, and how to keep your booking flexible. In short: if you have asthma or another lung or heart condition, choose another month. If you want to train in the north with the least risk, aim for November to January instead.

The south has its own hot, dry stretch at this time of year, and it does not have the same burning-season issue. That is one honest reason to pick Phuket or Samui for a March trip, and it is a fair choice if the air matters most to you.

## May to October: rainy season, cheaper and quieter

The rainy season in Chiang Mai runs roughly from May to October. It rarely means rain all day. More often it is a clear morning followed by an afternoon downpour, with greener hills and fewer visitors. **Training in the rainy season** works well if you are flexible: mornings are usually the best window, and afternoon sessions sometimes get moved or shortened.

The upside is real. Camps are quieter, you get more attention, and accommodation and flights tend to be cheaper than in peak season. The downside is variability. Some weeks are lovely, others bring heavy rain for days.

A current note, dated 29 September 2026: Chiang Mai had not been flooded at that date, although heavy rain was affecting other parts of Thailand, and the Thai Meteorological Department had warned of heavy rain across the country, north included, from 5 to 10 October. We are not reusing older flood reports from other years, because conditions change every season. If you are travelling in the wet months, check the forecast a few days before you fly and keep some flexibility.

The south does not follow the same calendar. Phuket and the Andaman coast are at their wettest during these months, while Koh Samui gets its heaviest rain from mid-October to early December and is generally drier than Phuket through the middle of the year. If the north is showery, that is not a reason to assume the south is sunny.

## Best time by goal, and how long to stay now that visa-free is 30 days

The right month also depends on what you want out of the trip.

- **Weight loss and fitness.** Heat helps you sweat but hurts recovery. November to February is easiest to sustain, because you can train twice a day without exhausting yourself. In the hot months, stick to early sessions and drink more water than feels reasonable.
- **First fight.** Give yourself time. A first bout usually needs several weeks of consistent training, so pick a window with steady weather and avoid the peak of the burning season or a very heavy rainy stretch.
- **A long stay.** If you want two or three months, the rainy season is often the best value, and you can shift your dates around any bad patch.

Length of stay matters more than it used to. Since 15 September 2026, most nationalities, including the EU, UK and USA, get a 30-day visa exemption instead of 60 days, according to the Tourism Authority of Thailand. Press reports citing embassies say it can be extended once by 30 days at immigration for 1,900 THB, but that is at the officer's discretion, so do not count on it. Check with your embassy before you book, and read [how long you can stay visa-free and which visa fits](/blog/thailand-visa-guide-by-training-length) for the options beyond a month.

## FAQ: when to train Muay Thai in Thailand

### What is the best month for a beginner?

For Chiang Mai, December or January. The weather is at its friendliest and the mornings are cool. Book early, because that is also when everyone else wants to come.

### Chiang Mai or Phuket: which is better?

It depends on the month. The north is at its best from November to February, and Phuket from around December to March. Outside those windows the answer changes, so pick the region that matches your dates.

### Can you train Muay Thai in the rainy season?

Yes. Rain usually comes in bursts, so mornings tend to be more reliable. You get quieter camps and lower prices, in exchange for less predictable weather.

### Is Chiang Mai too hot to train?

March and April are hot, and the air quality can be poor. November to February is far more comfortable. Our camp is open-air, so we feel the weather, and we adapt session times when we need to.

### How far ahead should I book?

As early as you can for November to February. We do not give a fixed number of weeks, because demand changes each year. If you have set dates, message us and we will tell you honestly what that period looks like.

## Conclusion

The best month depends on your priorities. For comfort, choose November to February in the north. For a quieter and cheaper trip, consider the rainy season. For March and April, be honest with yourself about heat and air quality, and think about the south. And with a 30-day visa exemption now, decide how long you want to stay before you pick your dates.

The real **DTV visa cost** is not 10,000 THB. That number is real, but it is only one of three layers, and most people only find out about the other two after they have already started the process.

Here's the honest total: a typical Muay Thai candidate pays somewhere between **17,500 and 35,000 THB** once you add the embassy fee and a soft power training course together. A smaller group spends far more on top of that, usually on an agent they didn't need. No single "10,000 THB" number tells you that.

I'm Meaw. I own and run Wildcat Muay Thai in Chiang Mai, and I write the soft power enrolment documents myself for every trainee who needs one for their DTV visa cost planning. I see candidates every month who thought they were done paying after the embassy fee, and then got surprised by the course price, or paid an agent they didn't actually need. This article breaks down all three layers with real numbers, so you can budget for the whole thing before you start. If you haven't already, it's worth reading [the full DTV visa guide](/dtv-visa) for eligibility and process first, this article stays focused on cost.

## Layer 1: the embassy visa fee

The DTV embassy application fee is a flat **10,000 THB, and it is not refundable** if your application is rejected, according to [The Thaiger's DTV guide](https://thethaiger.com/guides/visa-information/thailand-digital-nomad-visa). It's the one number every guide gets right, and the one most people mistake for the full cost.

You pay this fee when you submit your application through the [official Thailand e-Visa portal](https://thaievisa.go.th), the only system the government runs for this visa. Each embassy converts that 10,000 THB into its own local currency, which is why the real-world cost varies by country. Across different embassies, applicants have reported an equivalent **ranging from about 275 to 1,150 USD**, depending on the exchange rate and fee structure the embassy applies. Quite a spread for what is, on paper, a single fixed fee.

Some applicants have mentioned the fee translating into a few hundred euros when filing through a European embassy, which sits comfortably inside that wider confirmed range rather than as an outlier. Whatever the local currency comes out to, the fee itself does not change with your nationality, your soft power activity, or the length of your training program.

One detail worth knowing: you can only apply for the DTV from **outside Thailand**, at an embassy or consulate in your home country or country of residence. There is no way to convert a tourist visa into a DTV from inside the country, so this fee has to be paid before you travel, not after.

## Layer 2: the soft power course, Muay Thai in this case

Muay Thai is [one of the officially recognised soft power activities](https://www.muaythaivisathailand.com/soft-power-and-the-destination-thailand-visa-for-muay-thai-students/) that qualify you for the DTV, and the course built around it typically costs **between 7,500 and 25,000 THB** for the same basic paperwork: an enrolment letter, and sometimes an attendance record. That is more than triple the price at the top end, for documents that are essentially the same one or two pages.

The gap exists mostly because of how gyms bundle the price, not because the paperwork itself is more complex somewhere. Some camps only issue DTV documents alongside a mandatory long training package, often six months paid upfront, which pushes the number up even if you only plan to train a month before you leave to apply. Others simply charge a separate "DTV processing fee" on top of their normal training rate for the same one-page letter. We covered this in detail in [our breakdown of gyms overcharging for DTV papers](/blog/muay-thai-gym-dtv-overcharging), including the exact signals that tell a fair price from an inflated one. Muay Thai isn't your only soft power option either, if you're weighing the cost against alternatives, see [our comparison of the cheapest DTV soft power activities](/blog/cheapest-dtv-soft-power-activity).

At Wildcat, the enrolment letter is included in the normal training price, with no separate DTV line item: **5,000 THB a month** for unlimited group training, or **7,000 THB a month** for the Fighter program. If you want the fuller picture of what training here for your DTV actually looks like, see [the DTV visa for Muay Thai training](/dtv-visa/muay-thai).

## Layer 3: do you need an agent? Usually not

Most DTV applicants do not need a visa agent, and paying one is the single biggest way this cost balloons. A DTV agent typically charges **between 30,000 and 80,000 THB**, on top of everything above.

Some applicants report current agent rates starting around 30,000 THB, rising well past that depending on how much handling they expect. For a complete file with the right proof of funds, a real enrolment letter, and a coherent plan, the application process is direct enough that most people handle it themselves. An agent adds value mainly when your paperwork is genuinely unusual, not as a default step everyone should budget for.

## Putting it all together: total cost by scenario

Here is what the three layers add up to in practice, without an agent involved for most people:

- **Minimal scenario** (no agent, one-month course): roughly **17,500 to 35,000 THB** total, combining the 10,000 THB embassy fee with a 7,500 to 25,000 THB soft power course.
- **With an agent added**: add **30,000 to 80,000 THB** on top of the minimal scenario, pushing the total closer to **47,500 to 115,000 THB** for applicants who choose that route.

One thing this total does not include: your **monthly cost of living** once you're actually in Thailand, rent, food, transport, and ongoing training fees. That is a separate budget entirely, and mixing the two numbers together is how people underestimate what a long stay really costs. For that full picture, see [the complete monthly budget for training and living long-term](/dtv-visa/long-stay-training).

## Is the DTV visa worth the cost?

For most people planning several months or years of training and travel in Thailand, the one-time cost of a DTV compares well against a visa that stays valid for **5 years, multiple-entry**, at 180 days per stay. That's not a guarantee the visa gets approved, or that it fits every budget. But as a one-shot expense against years of access, the math tends to favor the DTV over repeated short-term arrangements.

That said, for genuinely short trips, a tourist visa with border runs can sometimes work out cheaper, since you skip the soft power course entirely. We break down that comparison, and when it actually makes sense, in [DTV visa versus tourist visa runs on cost](/blog/dtv-vs-tourist-visa-runs-cost).

As Meaw Boonpradub, who owns Wildcat and prepares these enrolment documents herself, puts it: "Most candidates think they're finished paying once the embassy fee clears. They're not being careless, they just haven't been told the other two layers exist yet."

## FAQ on the DTV visa cost

### What's the minimum you can spend on a DTV visa?

The minimum realistic total is around **17,500 THB**: the 10,000 THB embassy fee plus the lowest end of the soft power course range, roughly 7,500 THB, with no agent involved. That covers the visa application itself, not your living costs once you arrive.

### Is the DTV visa fee refundable if I'm rejected?

No. The 10,000 THB embassy application fee is non-refundable whether your DTV is approved or rejected. That is one reason a complete, well-documented file matters before you submit, not after.

### Do I need a visa agent to apply for the DTV?

Usually not. Most applicants with a complete file, proof of funds, a real soft power enrolment, and a coherent plan, handle the DTV application directly. Agents typically cost 30,000 to 80,000 THB and tend to add the most value for genuinely unusual cases, not as a routine step.

### Does the DTV visa cost cover my living expenses in Thailand?

No. Everything in this breakdown is the one-time cost of the visa itself: the embassy fee, the soft power course, and an optional agent. Rent, food, transport, and ongoing training once you're in Thailand are a separate monthly budget, covered in [our full cost-of-living breakdown](/dtv-visa/long-stay-training).

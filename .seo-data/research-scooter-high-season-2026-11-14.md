## 3. What to watch for (verified)
Passport as deposit
- Governments say do not hand it over. Irish Dept of Foreign Affairs page ("Still current at: 14 November 2026", updated 11 Nov 2026): "Do not hand over your passport as security for vehicle hire." Embassy "cannot retrieve your passport for you." Also "We strongly recommend not to rent a scooter or motorcycle while in Thailand." UK FCDO page (no date shown; latest item 28 Aug 2026): "Do not hand your passport over as a guarantee, for example to motorcycle or jet-ski rental businesses." CONFIRMED (2 official). US State Dept and Smartraveller fetches failed (403/timeout); their wording is only known via a search snippet and via Byklo (secondary) = UNVERIFIED by me directly.
- Legality: Byklo (rental platform, 2026-10-27): "There is no Thai statute or regulation that prohibits a private rental business from holding a foreign passport." Chiang Mai Ambassador (May 2026): "technically against Thai regulations but remains common in practice." CONTRADICTORY; do not state that it is illegal. Advice not to do it is solid; legality claim is UNVERIFIED.
- Practice: Mango page: "Physical passport can be left at the shop with no cash deposit if preferred" (shop's own page). Catmotors/chiangmaiscooterrental/fleetcnx: copy only, "never keep your passport." So practice is mixed.
Cash deposit norms (shop pages): Cat Motors 1,000/3,000/5,000/8,000/10,000 by model; Mango 1,000 (125cc), 2,000 (150), 4,000 (300); Fleetcnx 2,000 (110-160cc), 5,000 CB300, 20,000 ADV350; Skoot 3,000-10,000; Chiang Mai Motorbike Rentals "NO DEPOSIT" (monthly). Byklo: 1,000-3,000 (110-125cc), 4,000-6,000 (300-350cc). Range CONFIRMED: ~1,000-5,000 for a normal scooter, scales with bike value.
Photo/video and contract: Byklo (2026-10-27): "Photograph or film the bike in a slow walkaround before you accept the keys... with shop staff visible"; get deposit figure written on the agreement. Ride Chiang Mai: photograph with staff at handover. Chiang Mai Ambassador: receipt plus written confirmation of refund. Practice advice, not law. CONFIRMED as consistent advice (3 commercial sources).
Rental insurance / Por Ror Bor
- Por Ror Bor (compulsory, injury only, not the bike): Motorist.co.th (2026-01-14): per person, preliminary: medical up to 30,000; death/disability 35,000; max (after fault proven, non-at-fault party): medical 80,000, death/total disability 500,000, inpatient 200/day up to 20 days. "covers only bodily injury and death"; no property damage or vehicle repair. Thaiger page (updated 2026-09-10, "rates directional") gives overlapping 30,000/80,000/500,000 figures, ambiguous wording. Riders Corner: "does not cover damage to the bike itself." CONFIRMED (3). Fleetcnx: covers "up to 30,000 THB for the rider and up to 80,000 for a passenger" (shop wording; differs, do not reuse).
- Change 2026: OIC Registrar Orders 16/2569 and 17/2569 (7 Aug 2026), per Howden (2026-09-17): cancellation/policy-period rules, new duration rules effective 2026-09-01. Page gives no change to limits. Not relevant to renters; limits change UNVERIFIED.
- Shop add-ons vary: Catmotors "medical insurance, up to 300,000 baht per driver and passenger" (no damage cover stated); Chiang Mai Motorbike Rentals "Class A insurance"; Ride Chiang Mai: Bikago USD 95 damage excess; Big Dog "scratch cover is a shop policy rather than formal insurance." Ask for the excess in baht. SINGLE-SOURCE each.
- Travel insurance and licence: UK FCDO: riding without a valid licence "could invalidate your travel insurance." Irish DFA: no helmet "may not be covered by your insurance." Thaiger IDP guide (2026-03-20): car-only IDP means insurers may treat you as unlicensed and deny claims. CONFIRMED.
Helmet law
- Section 122 Land Traffic Act; Royal Thai Police "Safe Roads Project" from 1 June 2025: riders and passengers, fine up to 2,000 baht; doubles if passenger without helmet (Nation 2025-05-28; Thaiger 2025-05-27; Time Out Chiang Mai 2025-05-30: up to B2,000, "can also double to B4,000 if both rider and passenger are not wearing helmets"). CONFIRMED. Older figure 500 baht still appears on Riders Corner (undated 2026), so it is stale. Chiang Rai Times says 4,000 if helmet carried but not worn: SINGLE-SOURCE, not echoed by Nation/Thaiger/Time Out; do not use. No Chiang Mai-specific enforcement in Time Out.
Licence
- Irish DFA: "An international or Thai driving licence is required to drive in Thailand." UK FCDO: carry "the 1968 version of the international driving permit (IDP) and your UK driving licence." Thaiger (2026-03-20): Thailand recognised 1949 Geneva in 1962 and added 1968 Vienna in 2020; "You need a specific Category A motorcycle endorsement on both your national licence and your IDP"; car-only IDP does not cover scooters; ~90-day tourist threshold, longer stays or non-immigrant visa = Thai licence (some shops/insurers use 60 days). Riders Corner: IDP "must include the motorcycle category", only cites 1949 Geneva. Conventions: both accepted in practice per Thaiger; "which is formally recognised" conflicts across blogs, so state: carry the IDP matching your country's convention with the motorcycle category plus home motorcycle licence. Primary DLT/embassy text NOT found. Motorcycle-category requirement CONFIRMED (3 sources, none primary Thai); 90-day threshold SINGLE-SOURCE.
- Minimum rental age 18: Riders Corner only (SINGLE-SOURCE).
Fines and checkpoints
- Licence fine amounts: blogs say 500-2,000 THB (Thaiger guide "500 to 2,000"; Riders Corner table "No or invalid licence 500-2,000, possible impoundment"; others 500-1,000). No official source fetched; present as "reported 500-2,000". Checkpoints "common" around Chiang Mai, typical checks: licence, helmet, green book (Riders Corner; SINGLE-SOURCE, no date).
- 2025-2026 changes: helmet fine rise (above) is the only confirmed one. Thai Examiner (2026-11-08): DLT draft licensing amendments published 2026-11-04, consultation to 2026-12-04, NOT in force; would remove exemptions for foreigners converting overseas licences to Thai licences; "does not introduce new penalties for speeding, drink-driving or failing to wear motorcycle helmets"; no new restrictions on rental businesses or on tourists renting; IDPs for visitors not explicitly affected. SINGLE-SOURCE (news summary, not the draft). Blog claims of a Chiang Mai crackdown, 20,901 Phuket foreign offenders in 2025, and 10,000-100,000 THB fines on rental operators: UNVERIFIED (not found in any official or news source; the Thai Examiner piece contradicts the operator point).
- UK FCDO: "Motorcycles and scooters for hire in tourist areas are often unregistered and cannot be used legally on a public road." SINGLE-SOURCE official; useful: ask for the green book.
Fuel, theft, return: Ride Chiang Mai: most shops full-to-full, refuel fee 100-200 THB; Riders Corner: leaves with full tank, "return as received". Locks/theft: nothing verified beyond Monthly Chiang Mai advice to check theft liability (SINGLE-SOURCE).
Shop-run tourist scam context: Byklo claims US Embassy says the damage scam concentrates in Phuket/Pattaya, not the north: UNVERIFIED (page not fetched directly).

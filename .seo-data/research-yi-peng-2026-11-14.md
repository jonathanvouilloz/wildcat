## 4. Terminology and culture
| Claim | Status | Source | Consulted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yi = "two", peng = "full moon day": full moon of the 2nd month of the Lanna lunar calendar, = 12th month of Central Thai calendar; usually November | CONFIRMED (Thailand Foundation, 24 Nov 2023; Wikipedia) | https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518234 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Peng_festival | 2026-11-14 |
| khom loi = floating/sky lantern (fabric or paper on bamboo/wire frame, heated air); khom fai = decorative lanterns for houses/gardens/temples (Wikipedia); khom khwaen = hanging lantern | CONFIRMED Wikipedia (secondary). NOTE: Thailand Foundation uses "khom loy (also khom fai)" for the sky lantern, so the terms are used inconsistently; flag in the article | same | |
| Krathong = small decorated float on water (Loy Krathong); Lanna people traditionally used the sapao (sailing-ship float), later adopted the banana-stalk krathong | CONFIRMED (Thailand Foundation; Wikipedia: "small decorated rafts") | same | |
| Origin: Lan Na Kingdom (c.13th-18th C), possibly earlier Haripuñjaya (Lamphun); mainly Buddhist; merit-making ("tham bun"); releasing khom loi = letting go of past misfortune, wishes for next year, honouring ancestors | CONFIRMED (Wikipedia, Thailand Foundation) | same | |
| Phra Mae Khongkha (Ganga), water goddess; people pay respect and apologise for polluting water | CONFIRMED (Wikipedia; Thailand Foundation lists her among powers honoured) | same | |
| Yi Peng directs light up, Loy Krathong sends rafts onto water; Chiang Mai holds them as one multi-day event | CONFIRMED (Wikipedia) | same | |

### H2 — Eligibility & the DTV basics
*(intro 1 ligne : "The questions people ask before anything else.")*

**Q1. What is the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)?**
The DTV is a 5-year, multiple-entry Thailand visa that lets you stay up to 180 days per entry. It's built for remote workers ("Workcation") and people doing recognised Thai "soft power" activities like Muay Thai, Thai cooking or medical treatment. You apply once and use it over five years.

**Q2. Who is eligible for the DTV visa?**
Anyone who fits one of three categories: remote workers/freelancers for foreign employers (Workcation), participants in Thai soft-power activities (Muay Thai, cooking, medical care, festivals), or dependents of a DTV holder. You also need to show **500,000 THB** in funds. Specific paperwork depends on your category.

**Q3. Which countries can apply for the DTV?**
The DTV is open to most nationalities — there's no short "eligible countries" list the way some visas have. What changes by country is *where* and *how* you apply (your nearest Thai embassy/consulate or the e-Visa portal) and sometimes the exact documents requested. `[À VÉRIFIER]` Always confirm with the embassy you'll apply at.

**Q4. What are the DTV soft-power categories (Muay Thai, cooking, medical)?**
"Soft power" covers Thai cultural and wellness activities the government wants to promote — Muay Thai training, Thai cooking courses, medical treatment, sports and festivals. For each, you show you're genuinely enrolled or booked. For Muay Thai, that means a long-term enrolment at a camp.

**Q5. Do I qualify for the DTV through Muay Thai training?**
Yes — Muay Thai is one of Thailand's recognised soft-power activities, so a long-term enrolment at a camp is a qualifying activity. You support it with an enrolment/training letter from the camp. *Full guide → `/dtv-visa/muay-thai`.*

**Q6. What is a soft-power acceptance / enrolment letter, and who issues it?**
It's a letter from your camp or school confirming you're enrolled in a long-term Muay Thai program — one of the documents that backs your soft-power application. The camp issues it; at Wildcat, Meaw prepares it to match your actual training plan. It supports your file but doesn't replace the other requirements (funds, passport, application).

**Q7. Is the DTV easy to get? What are common denial reasons?**
For a well-prepared file it's a straightforward visa, but it's not automatic. The denials we hear about usually come from weak proof of funds (money that just appeared, no history), a soft-power/remote-work activity that looks thin or generic, or inconsistent documents. Honest answer: a clean, consistent file is everything. *Full eligibility guide → `/dtv-visa/eligibility`.*

**Q8. Is there a minimum age for the DTV?**
The main applicant must be an adult (18+). `[À VÉRIFIER]` Children can come as **dependents** on a DTV Dependent visa. Check the exact rule with your embassy if a minor is the main applicant.

### H2 — Money & funds
*(intro: "The 500,000 THB question, and everything around it.")*

**Q9. How much money do I need in the bank for the DTV?**
You need to show **500,000 THB** (roughly USD 14,500–17,000) in a bank statement. This is the financial requirement across categories. It proves you can support yourself during long stays.

**Q10. How long must the 500,000 THB sit in my account?**
Plan for the money to be there with some history, not deposited the day before you apply. `[À VÉRIFIER]` exact seasoning periods aren't fixed publicly and embassies read statements differently — a stable balance over several months is the safe read. When in doubt, ask the embassy you'll apply at.

**Q11. Can a sponsor in Thailand cover the funds requirement?**
Honest answer: it depends on the embassy and category, so don't assume yes. `[À VÉRIFIER]` Some posts accept a sponsorship letter plus the sponsor's funds; others want the money in the applicant's own account. Confirm before you build your file around a sponsor.

**Q12. How much does the DTV visa cost?**
The visa fee is around **USD 400** (about 10,000 THB), though it varies by embassy. That's separate from the funds you show and from any in-country extension fee. Budget for the fee per applicant.

**Q13. Do dependents pay a separate visa fee?**
Yes — each dependent's DTV is a separate application with its own fee. `[À VÉRIFIER]` exact amount by embassy. Build the per-person cost into your family budget.

**Q14. Do I have to show the funds again when I extend in-country?**
Possibly — extensions are handled by Thai immigration, not the embassy, and requirements can differ. `[À VÉRIFIER]` Keep your bank statements current so you're ready either way.

### H2 — Applying for the DTV
*(intro: "Where, how, and how long it takes.")*

**Q15. How do I apply for the DTV visa?**
You apply online through Thailand's official e-Visa portal (thaievisa.go.th) **from outside Thailand** — create an account, upload your documents, pay the fee. Some applicants use their nearest Thai embassy/consulate. *Step-by-step guide → `/dtv-visa/how-to-apply`.*

**Q16. Do I apply from outside Thailand?**
Yes. The DTV must be applied for from **outside** Thailand — you can't convert to it from inside the country on the same trip. Plan to apply before you travel, or on a trip home.

**Q17. What documents do I need for the DTV application?**
A passport, proof of 500,000 THB in funds, and documents for your category — for Muay Thai, your camp enrolment/training letter; for Workcation, proof of remote work. Exact lists vary by embassy and category. We provide the training documents; the rest stays on your side and we guide you through it.

**Q18. How long does DTV processing take?**
It varies a lot — from a few days to several weeks — depending on the embassy and how complete your file is. `[À VÉRIFIER]` There's no single official figure. A complete, consistent file is the best way to keep it short.

**Q19. Why does processing time vary so much between embassies?**
Each Thai embassy/consulate handles its own DTV workload and applies the document rules slightly differently. Busy posts are slower; some request extra paperwork. That's why we tell people to check their specific embassy's page and apply with margin before they want to travel.

**Q20. Do I need a visa agent for the DTV?**
No — many people apply themselves through the e-Visa portal. The real value isn't an agent, it's getting your category documents right; for the Muay Thai route, that's where the camp comes in. We're not a visa agency — we give you the training documents and guide you, and you keep control of your own file.

### H2 — The 180-day rule, extensions & re-entry
*(intro: "How the 5 years and the 180 days actually work.")*

**Q21. How long can I stay per entry on the DTV?**
Up to **180 days per entry**. The visa itself is valid 5 years with multiple entries, so you can come and go across those five years. Each fresh entry starts a new 180-day stay.

**Q22. What happens after 180 days — can I extend?**
You can extend once inside Thailand for another **180 days** (extension fee around **1,900 THB**), or do a visa run. After that you reset by leaving and re-entering. Extensions are handled by Thai immigration.

**Q23. What is a visa run and how does it reset my stay?**
A visa run is simply leaving Thailand and re-entering on your still-valid DTV, which starts a fresh 180-day stay. Because the DTV is multiple-entry for 5 years, you can do this throughout its validity. `[À VÉRIFIER]` whether posts limit how often — plan trips that make sense, not back-to-back border bounces.

**Q24. Do I need to do a 90-day report on the DTV?**
If you stay in Thailand for more than 90 consecutive days, you generally file a 90-day address report with immigration — it's an address notification, not a new visa. `[À VÉRIFIER]` exact handling for DTV holders with your local immigration office.

**Q25. Do I need a re-entry permit with the DTV?**
No — the DTV is **multiple-entry**, so you don't need a separate re-entry permit to leave and come back during its validity. That's one of its big advantages over single-entry long-stay visas.

**Q26. How do I keep my DTV active across the 5 years?**
Keep the visa valid by using it within its 5-year window and respecting each 180-day stay (extend or exit/re-enter as needed). Rules around long-stay visas do change, so check current requirements before each trip. *(News changes often — we keep this page's review date current.)*

### H2 — Tax
*(intro: "Living here long-term means thinking about tax. Short version, then talk to a tax pro.")*
*Disclaimer inline obligatoire (cf. §8).*

**Q27. Will I owe Thai tax on the DTV?**
Maybe — it depends on how long you stay and whether you bring foreign income into Thailand. Spending **180+ days in a calendar year** makes you a Thai tax resident, which can create Thai tax obligations on income remitted to Thailand. This is general info, not tax advice — talk to a tax professional about your situation.

**Q28. Does the 180-day rule make me a Thai tax resident?**
Yes — **180 days or more in a calendar year** in Thailand makes you a tax resident for that year, separate from your visa's 180-day-per-entry stay limit. They're two different "180s." Tax residency can mean filing and paying Thai tax on certain income.

**Q29. Do I still have to file taxes back home (e.g. US)?**
Usually yes — your home-country obligations don't disappear because you're on the DTV. US citizens, for example, generally still file (FEIE, FBAR may apply). `[À VÉRIFIER]` your own country's rules — this isn't tax advice; speak to a cross-border tax professional.

### H2 — Family & dependents

**Q30. Can I bring my spouse and children on the DTV?**
Yes — your spouse and children under 20 can apply as **dependents** on linked DTV Dependent visas. Each dependent files their own application with their own fee. You'll show proof of relationship.

**Q31. Do dependents need their own work or funds documents?**
Dependents don't need their own remote-work or soft-power activity — they qualify through their relationship to you. `[À VÉRIFIER]` whether each dependent must also show funds, or whether the main applicant's 500,000 THB covers the family — confirm with your embassy.

### H2 — Working & training on the DTV

**Q32. Can I work in Thailand on the DTV?**
You can work **remotely for foreign employers or clients** — that's the whole point of the Workcation category. You **cannot** take a Thai job, work for Thai companies, or invoice Thai clients, and you don't get a Thai work permit. The DTV is not a work-permit visa.

**Q33. Is the DTV the right visa for a multi-month Muay Thai trip?**
For a serious multi-month training stay, the DTV is usually the best fit — 180 days per entry, 5-year validity, and Muay Thai is a qualifying soft-power activity. We help you with the training documents and guide your file. *Plan your stay → `/dtv-visa/long-stay-training`.*

## 5. Spec — filtre de recherche live

**Comportement**
- Un `<input type="search">` en haut de la liste FAQ ("Search the FAQ — type a keyword like *tax*, *180 days*, *funds*…").
- Filtre **live** sur le **texte de la question ET de la réponse** (match insensible à la casse, sur le `textContent` de chaque `<details>`).
- **Debounce ~150–200 ms** sur l'event `input` (évite de filtrer à chaque frappe).
- Chaque `<details>` non-matché reçoit `hidden` (ou `display:none`). Les `<h2>` de thème dont toutes les questions sont masquées se masquent aussi (state vide par section).
- **Compteur** mis à jour : `"{n} questions"` / `"1 question"` / `"No questions match \"{q}\""`.
- **État vide** : si 0 match, afficher un bloc CTA : *"No match for that one — ask us directly. We reply on WhatsApp."* + bouton WhatsApp (`https://wa.me/66857209620`) + lien `/contact`.
- **Reset** : croix native du `type="search"` (ou bouton clear) → ré-affiche tout, compteur total.
- Quand l'input matche, **ouvrir** automatiquement les `<details>` matchés (`open`) pour montrer la réponse surlignée ; refermer au reset.

**a11y**
- `<input>` avec `<label>` visible ou `aria-label`.
- Compteur dans un conteneur `aria-live="polite"` (annonce "12 questions" après filtrage).
- État vide annoncé via le même `aria-live`.
- Le filtre est un **enhancement progressif** : sans JS, tous les `<details>` sont visibles et fonctionnels (la FAQPage JSON-LD reste valide). Îlot Astro vanilla JS, pas de framework.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (pas d'animation d'ouverture si réduit).

**Note SEO** : le filtre masque visuellement mais **ne supprime jamais** le DOM → toutes les Q/R restent dans le HTML rendu (indexables) et dans le JSON-LD.

**Recommandation (option la plus propre) — Option A :**

- **Bonus dédup contenu** : reformuler les 5 Q du pillar pour qu'elles ne soient pas identiques aux Q de la satellite (le pillar = teaser orienté Wildcat/camp ; la satellite = exhaustif neutre). Pas obligatoire pour le schema une fois le FAQPage retiré du pillar, mais propre pour l'UX et le maillage.

**Pourquoi A plutôt que de différencier les questions** : différencier 5 questions tout en gardant deux FAQPage reste fragile (Google peut toujours associer les deux à la même entité de silo). Concentrer le schema sur **une** page canonique FAQ est l'option la plus robuste et la plus alignée avec l'intent : `/dtv-visa/faq` EST la page FAQ.
